## Core results

1. **Tool output is ~63 % of a coding trajectory, and the trajectory is re-paid every step.**
   Measured on SWE-bench Verified trajectories collected from **Trae Agent on Claude 4 Sonnet**
   (§2.1; 100 of them manually inspected in §2.2): average = **48.4 K tokens in 40 steps**, of
   which **tool messages
   30.4 K**, assistant messages 13.7 K (**11.9 K of that is tool-call *arguments***), system+user
   **4.4 K**. Because every token is re-sent on every subsequent prediction, **accumulated input per
   issue = 1.0 M tokens** — a ~21× snowball off a 48 K final trajectory. Context: on OpenRouter in
   Sept 2025, Claude 4 Sonnet moved 100 B tokens/day of which **99 % were input** (§1).
2. **Most of what a long step contains is removable.** Across the five reduction LLMs tried, the
   reflection module kept only **Keep% = 14.4 %–29.0 %** of the steps it processed (Table 2), i.e.
   **71 %–86 % judged waste**; in the main evaluation **Keep% = 22.6 %–30.8 %**, i.e. **69.2 %–77.4 %
   removed** (Table 4, §5.2.1). This is the paper's only quantification of "how much is waste" — an
   LLM's judgement on steps > 500 tokens, not a ground-truth label, and not broken down by category.
3. **Headline savings, measured (Table 4, 4 arms).** Input tokens **−39.9 %–−59.7 %** (I = 0.601 /
   0.591 / 0.596 / 0.403); agent-side cost **−28.6 %–−44.1 %**; **after** the reflection module's own
   cost (`$+` = 0.055–0.118) **final cost −21.1 %–−35.9 %**. Absolute cost per instance: SbV+Claude 4
   Sonnet **$0.535 → $0.422**; SbV+Gemini 2.5 Pro **$0.385 → $0.285**; MSbF+Claude **$1.277 →
   $0.933**; MSbF+Gemini **$0.701 → $0.449**. Cost falls less than input tokens because of output
   tokens and **invalidated KV cache** (their `$` already prices the cached-input discount).
4. **Resolve rate: flat, with no significance test.** Pass% Original → AgentDiet: SbV+Claude
   **64.5 → 66.5**; SbV+Gemini **50.5 → 52.0**; MSbF+Claude **40.0 → 39.0**; MSbF+Gemini **21.7 →
   22.7** — the authors' range **−1.0 %–+2.0 %** (Finding 2). N = 200 (SbV), 300 (MSbF), so ±2 points
   ≈ 4–6 instances. **No confidence interval, no significance test, no repeated runs anywhere in the
   paper.** "Maintains the same performance" is a parity claim from single-seed point estimates plus
   the Step/PStep corroborator, not a statistical result.
5. **Steps do not increase — the second, better safety signal.** Step / PStep (avg steps for all /
   for resolved instances) barely move: SbV+Claude 39.62 → 39.95 and 37.75 → 38.21; SbV+Gemini 37.98
   → 37.44 and 32.59 → 31.72; MSbF+Claude 70.37 → 70.45 and 62.08 → 60.77. An increase would mean the
   agent had to re-fetch what was removed; it does not appear.
6. **The biggest win came from a degradation case, not from savings.** MSbF + **Gemini 2.5 Pro**:
   steps **57.20 → 43.90**, PStep **37.86 → 29.75**, input tokens −59.7 %. Cause (§5.2.2, Fig. 5):
   Gemini "shows increasingly abnormal behavior when the context is too long, often resulting in
   repeated invalid tool calls until the step limit"; reduction cut instances hitting the 100-step
   limit from **66 to 26**. Presented as a robustness side-effect, conjecturally attributed to
   long-context degradation (they cite *Lost in the Middle*, LongCodeU, *LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn
   Conversation*) — the mechanism is not itself measured.
7. **Agents will not compress themselves.** They built an `erase` tool letting the agent replace a
   numbered trajectory segment with a takeaway (`{"id": 17, "takeaway": "unrelated content"}`).
   **Both Claude 4 Sonnet and Gemini 2.5 Pro ignored it**, even under a system prompt saying
   "ONLY call the 'erase' tool and DO NOT continue working on the original task" (transcript,
   django__django-13012: the model answers "Let me continue by examining more of the Expression class
   code…" and calls other tools). Conjectured cause: the repair procedure is memorised in training.
   Consequence: reduction must be **external**, and the agent is kept **unaware** of it.
8. **Timing matters more than the reducer.** Reducing the step that just finished (**a = 0**) costs
   real performance: Pass **59** vs Original 65, Step **44.94** vs 39.74 (Table 3). Delaying by two
   steps (**a = 2**) restores Pass 65 at Step 38.90. Reducing later still (a = 3) is *safe but less
   profitable* (I 0.624 vs 0.586) — every token removed earlier is saved on all remaining turns.
9. **Blind deletion is cheap but worse; the guardrails, not the intelligence, do most of the work.**
   Baselines at a = 2, θ = 500 (Table 2): **Delete** (drop everything processed) Keep 0 %, I 0.428,
   $ 0.655, **Pass 58** (−7 pts), Step 45.43 (+14 %); **Random** (drop 75 %) Pass 64, Step 44.95;
   **LLMLingua-2** (trained compressor) Pass 61, Step 43.89, and it mangles structure — it destroys
   test method names (§7.1's listing shows its output as unreadable token soup). All still score
   ≥ 58 % because the window guardrails stop the reducer touching recent or short steps.

## Method and setting (what was actually built/tested)

**AgentDiet** = a *reflection module* bolted onto an otherwise unmodified agent loop (Algorithm 1).
At agent step *s* it may rewrite **only step s − a**, given context **steps s − a − b … s**; it
skips entirely if that step is ≤ **θ** tokens, and applies the rewrite only if it saves > θ tokens.
Tuned settings: **a = 2, b = 1, θ = 500, LLM_reflect = GPT-5 mini** (12× cheaper than Claude 4
Sonnet), found by hill-climbing on a held-out-from-eval subset of 100 SWE-bench Verified instances.
Rewriting only a near-tail step **preserves the KV cache of everything before it** and makes a
runaway reducer non-catastrophic.

**Removal is done by an LLM, prompted — not by heuristics.** Rules/regex were considered and
rejected: "it is difficult to write a rule to identify all verbose output in test logs, since every
project has its own output format" (§2.3.1). The prompt has four parts (Fig. 1 outline): job
description; I/O format (each step wrapped in `<step id="…">`); summary + examples of the three waste
kinds; and **guidelines to prevent information loss** — "Replace the text to remove with a short
takeaway"; "keep the original structure unchanged, e.g., XML tags". Guidelines were refined against
pilot-study waste instances used as test cases.

**Three waste categories** (§2.2, from manual inspection of 100 Trae/Claude 4 Sonnet trajectories):
*Useless* — repo-wide file listings returning `__pycache__`/`.egg-info`, verbose build/test output
(`make[2]: Entering directory …`), full PASSED lists; Trae already truncates each tool response at
16 KB and "a fixed threshold only avoids extreme cases". *Redundant* — `str_replace_editor` echoes
`new_str` back in its response; `new_str` shares lines with `old_str`; and `old_str` re-states code
the agent already read in an earlier step. *Expired* — grep-then-open-each-hit loops: "once the agent
identifies the faulty file, most content in other files becomes unnecessary". **No rule defines
"expired"** — the LLM decides from the surrounding b + a + 1 steps; the algorithm's only formal
levers are the delay *a* and the size floor θ.

**Scaffold/models/benchmarks.** Agent = **Trae Agent** (open source, #1 on SWE-bench Verified July
2025; tools `bash`, `str_replace_editor`, `think`, `task_done`); generalisation is *argued* from
mini-SWE-agent and OpenHands being "semantically similar", but **only Trae was instrumented**. Agent
models: **Claude 4 Sonnet** and **Gemini 2.5 Pro** (no Opus, no extended thinking mentioned).
Benchmarks: **SWE-bench Verified** — 100 instances for pilot + tuning, **200 randomly drawn from the
remaining 400** for evaluation, s_max = 50 — and **Multi-SWE-bench Flash** (300 instances, 7
non-Python languages, s_max = 100). Per-language results (Table 5) hold: ΔPass −3…+1 (Claude) and
−3…+4 (Gemini) instances out of 40–45 per language. Nothing is trained; everything is prompting plus
an external loop. Total experiment spend ≈ **US $2,000**.

**Wall-time is not measured** (§6.1.1): they refuse to compare latency because commercial API
latency is unstable under server load. They argue the added reflection call is cheap and the agent's
own calls get shorter, and note reflection can be run **in parallel** with the agent step at the
price of one step of context (a → a − 1). Step/PStep is the only time-like proxy reported.

## Relevance to P1–P4

**P1 (context is the cost).** Directly on point, same shape as our numbers: 99 % of Claude Sonnet's
public token traffic is input; tool messages are 63 % of a trajectory; a 48 K trajectory costs 1.0 M
accumulated input tokens. Their fix recovers 21–36 % of end cost **with caching already priced in** —
but only for something that can rewrite history. Their cost model is API list price with a
cached-input discount, not our 1.25×/0.1× write/read split; a rewrite in our loop would break the
cache from the rewrite point on, which is exactly why they only touch step s − 2.

**P2 (sessions grow; the longest dominate).** Result 6 is the strongest evidence here that *the
longest sessions are where both the waste and the failure live*: with Gemini, halving trajectory
length cut step-limit exhaustion from 66/300 to 26/300. Our doc-writer (145 K median, 438 K peak, 192
turns) is the analogue. Caveat: **Claude 4 Sonnet showed no such pathology** (Step 39.62 → 39.95,
70.37 → 70.45), so Claude-side evidence is "no harm", not "recovers a failing session".

**P3 (every session rebuilds the same picture).** Only obliquely. Their *redundant information*
category is the within-trajectory version of our cross-session re-reads (`old_str` re-states code
read earlier). AgentDiet does nothing about re-acquisition across sessions; §4.1 says applying it to
multi-agent systems means "adding the reflection module to all LLM agents" — untested, and each
trajectory is still compressed in isolation. No mechanism, no numbers for our P3.

**P4 (what smaller reads cost in grounding).** For *SWE-bench-style patching* the answer is "nothing
measurable at 70–77 % removal of long steps", with two instruments we could copy: **Pass%** (our
gate-red / rework share) and **Step/PStep** — re-fetch pressure, a sensitivity detector that needs no
ground truth, since an agent that lost something it needed goes and gets it again. But success here
is *tests pass*, not *the citation was right*: nothing measures whether a reduced trajectory still
produces accurate `file:line` references, and the reducer's guideline "keep the original structure
unchanged, e.g., XML tags" protects the step envelope, not line numbers inside a file view.

## Interventions this paper supports

- **Shape verbose tool output at the wrapper, before it enters context** (Result 2 + §2.2.1;
  Trae's flat 16 KB truncation is explicitly called insufficient). Filter `__pycache__`/`.egg-info`
  noise from listings, collapse passing-test lists to a count plus the failures, strip `make`
  directory chatter — the category the reducer most often emptied. **Grounding risk / gap:** the
  paper only removes content *after the agent has seen it in the turn it was produced*; a wrapper
  removes it *before*. That case is untested — their evidence does not license pre-emptive truncation
  of what the agent is reading right now.
- **Never trim the working set; trim only what is ≥ 2 steps old and > ~500 tokens** (Result 8: a = 0
  → Pass 59; θ sweep: θ = 0 → Pass 62, θ = 500 → Pass 65, θ = 2000 → Pass 58). For us a hand-off
  rule: what we drop at a session cut or in a sub-agent contract should be material the session has
  already acted on, and only when it is bulky.
- **Use a cheap model for the compression job, never the producing model** (Table 2 `$+` =
  5.2–14.8 % overhead; GPT-5 mini, 12× cheaper than the agent model, was *best* on both cost and
  Pass%). Where we already summarise — sub-agent returns, hand-off notes — the compressor need not be
  Opus; the settled "no weaker model as writer or reviewer" rule does not bite, because a compressor
  is neither.
- **Do not ask the agent to prune its own context** (Result 7). Any "summarise what you no longer
  need" instruction inside a producer prompt should be assumed inert; compression has to be a
  separate call the producing session never negotiates with.
- **Instrument with Step/PStep, not just Pass%** (Result 5; Delete's +14 % steps at −7 pts Pass).
  Turn count and repeat-read count per session are a cheaper, earlier over-trimming signal than
  blocking-finding rate. Costs nothing; closes part of P4.
- **Expect ~20–35 % end-cost relief, not 60 %** from any true in-session reduction (Result 3):
  input-token savings are roughly halved by output tokens, cache invalidation, and the reducer's bill.

## Applicability caveats

- **Model gap.** Claude 4 **Sonnet** and Gemini 2.5 Pro; no Opus, no extended-thinking configuration,
  and prior-turn reasoning traces are not in their token accounting. Our P4 note that thinking is
  5–12 % of context has no counterpart here.
- **Horizon gap.** 40 steps / 48 K trajectory / **$0.42–$1.28 per instance** versus our 20–200 turns,
  80–438 K per-turn context and $221 per slice — 200–500× apart in spend. The direction should hold
  *a fortiori*, but every number is from the small end.
- **Task gap.** Single-issue patch-until-tests-pass. No review task, no citation-accuracy task, no
  planning or documentation task, no multi-agent hand-off; success = held-out tests pass (they
  concede the plausible-vs-correct overfitting threat, §6.2.3).
- **Harness requirement.** The whole method mutates the trajectory data structure between agent
  turns — inapplicable to a session we cannot edit mid-flight. Their own framing: Claude Code's
  compaction is the *sparing* version of what they do *aggressively*.
- **Statistics.** No significance testing, no repeated runs, single-seed point estimates (Result 4).
  Hyperparameters were tuned on 100 instances and the reduction prompt refined on pilot cases from
  those same trajectories — the split protects the *test* set, but the prompt is SWE-bench-shaped.
- **One scaffold.** Trae Agent only; generalisation argued from tool similarity, not tested (§6.2.1,
  cost-constrained). Their dismissal of Lindenbauer et al. 2025 (simple observation masking) also
  deserves care: they equate it to their **Delete** baseline at a = 2, whereas that work masks tool
  messages only, after 10 steps — not the configuration they measured.

## Briefing check

The note — *"the measured outcome of removing stale tool output in software-engineering agents"* —
is supported, with two corrections.

Supported: the outcome is measured, on software-engineering agents, and it is a null on quality with
a real saving. Finding 2 verbatim: *"The ratio of successfully resolved instances for AgentDiet is
comparable (−1.0 %–+2.0 %) to that of the Original baseline. AgentDiet also does not cause the agent
to take more steps to resolve a task."* Finding 1: *"a reduction in input tokens by 39.9 %–59.7 %, or
a reduction in the final computational cost by 21.1 %–35.9 %."*

Correction 1 — **"stale" is one of three targets, and the least defined.** The categories are
*useless*, *redundant*, *expired*; the numbers are aggregate, so no share of the 69–77 % removal can
be attributed to staleness. The task's "two reduction operations" framing does not match the paper:
there is **one** operation (an LLM rewrite of one step) aimed at three kinds of waste, and "expired"
is decided by the reducing LLM from a 4-step window, not by any rule.

Correction 2 — **"removing" is really "rewriting in place".** Content is replaced by a short takeaway
inside the preserved step envelope, two steps after the fact, only above 500 tokens, and only when
the saving exceeds 500 tokens. Reading the note as licence to *drop* stale tool output is the
**Delete** baseline, which lost 7 points of Pass% (65 → 58) and added 14 % more steps. The safety
lives in the delay and the size floor at least as much as in the reducer's judgement.

**Transfer answer for the lead:** neither operation transfers *as published* to a Claude Code tool
loop — both are trajectory mutations, and the harness owns the trajectory. What transfers without the
harness is the *useless-information* target moved upstream, to where we do have control: tool-output
wrappers that never emit the noise, and hand-off/sub-agent contracts that carry a takeaway instead of
the raw material (their reduction is literally "replace with a short takeaway"). What **requires** the
harness is *expired*-information removal — deciding at turn 60 that the file view from turn 12 is
superseded is precisely an edit to history — and, as Result 7 shows, we cannot delegate that decision
to the producing session by prompting it: Claude ignored an explicit instruction to prune its own
context. Our file:line dependency is the one thing this paper does not cover: it never checks whether
a reduced trajectory still cites correctly.
