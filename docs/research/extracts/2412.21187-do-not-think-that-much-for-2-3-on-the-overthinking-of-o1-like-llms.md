## Core results

- o1-like models burn ~1,953% more tokens than conventional LLMs answering "2+3=?" (Intro; Fig. 1a). Their mitigation method cuts MATH500 output tokens by 48.6% on QwQ-32B-Preview with accuracy held (Intro; Fig. 1b).
- Solution-round distribution (§2.1, Fig. 2-3): QwQ/DeepSeek-R1 produce 2-4 solutions per response most of the time. Rounds fall with *increasing* difficulty despite tokens rising: MATH500 levels 1-2 average 3.7 (QwQ)/4.6 (R1) rounds vs. levels 4-5 average 3.0/3.9.
- Outcome efficiency (§2.2, Table "efficiency_main_results"): the first solution is already correct in >92% of cases and uses <60% of tokens; ξ_O (useful-token fraction) is only 41.9-52.3% for QwQ and 45.9-51.0% for DeepSeek-R1 across ASDIV/GSM8K/MATH500, vs. 92.6-96.3% for single-pass conventional LLMs. Level-1 (easiest) MATH500 problems score <50% outcome efficiency for both reasoning models (§2.4, Fig. 4) — overthinking is worst on easy items.
- Diversity (§2.3): distinctness ratio declines with solution index; solutions #≥4 average 11.5% less distinct than #3; solution #2 (the first "double-check") is markedly less distinct than #3 — later rounds mostly repeat earlier reasoning, not add new strategies.
- Mitigation (§3, Tables "training_data_efficiency"/"response_simplification"/"main_results"): self-training toward shortest-correct output (SimPO) cuts MATH500 tokens 22.3%; the adopted "FCS+Reflection" streaming (keep first-correct solution + one more) roughly halves-to-quarters tokens (e.g., AIME24: 9480.9→5154.5 tokens) while holding accuracy within ~0.1-3.4 points on ASDIV/GSM8K/GPQA/AIME24, and improving it on ASDIV/GSM8K. Aggressive truncation (FCS alone) *does* cost accuracy on hard MATH500 (93.0%→91.0%), showing harder problems need retained reflection.

## Relevance to A/B/C

- **A (deep-dive regardless of size)** — directly supported. Overthinking is empirically shown to concentrate on easy problems, and harder problems benefit from more reflection (§2.1, §2.4, §3.3) — the phenomenon our planner exhibits is exactly what this paper documents, in a different model family/task.
- **B (comment churn)** — none. No code, no comments, no claim-strength language studied.
- **C (work amplification via reviewers)** — none directly. The declining-distinctness finding (§2.3, later rounds repeat earlier ones) is a loose structural analogy to "re-raising similar findings," not evidence about multi-agent review loops.

## Supported interventions

- Cap "solution rounds" at first-complete-answer + one reflection pass, mirroring FCS+Reflection (§3.2-3.3), which preserved accuracy while cutting tokens substantially.
- Fine-tune/steer toward shortest-correct trajectories (SimPO > SFT/DPO/RPO, §3.1) if a training-based fix is ever viable.
- Use an ξ_O/ξ_P-style diagnostic (§2.2-2.3) — fraction of planner/coder output that actually contributed to the accepted final answer — to *measure* overthinking in our loop before deciding how to gate it.
- Target the "second-guess" pass specifically for trimming: solution #2 is the least distinct round (§2.3), suggesting reflexive double-checks are the lowest-value place to spend extra effort.

## Applicability caveats

Tested only on QwQ-32B-Preview/DeepSeek-R1 (32B-class open reasoning models) on closed-form math with scalar-checkable answers (ASDIV/GSM8K/MATH500/GPQA/AIME) — not Claude, not code/planning tasks, not open-ended correctness. The mitigation requires model fine-tuning (SFT/DPO/SimPO) via self-training, not a prompting/orchestration change, conflicting with a "no per-task tuning, single generic loop" constraint unless reframed as a prompting heuristic. Dec 2024 preprint, 32B single-model scale; no multi-agent reviewer loop is studied at all.

## Briefing check

Nuance, not full confirmation. "Disproportionate compute on easy problems" and "task-dependent" are strongly confirmed (§2.1, §2.4, §3.2-3.3). But "error accumulation in long chains" is **not** this paper's finding — it never shows extended thinking introducing or compounding errors; its failure mode is *redundancy/waste* (later solutions are correct-but-repetitive, not wrong), with the one accuracy cost being from *under*-thinking (FCS truncation hurts hard problems, §3.3), the opposite direction. Suggest correcting the briefing's "error accumulation" clause for this citation, or attributing it to a different source.
