## Core results

1. **Same instruction, split across turns, costs ~39 % of performance.** All 15 LLMs degraded on all
   six tasks from Full (single-turn, fully-specified) to Sharded (one shard of the instruction
   revealed per turn): **average degradation −39 %** (§6.1, Table `tab:main_results`; abstract
   agrees). The Introduction frames it absolutely — Sharded **65 %** vs Full **90 %**; the teaser
   figure says −35 %. The paper does not reconcile the three framings; −39 % is the Results number.
2. **The loss is not from rephrasing.** **Concat** — the same shards concatenated as bullets in one
   turn — averages **95.1 % of Full** (§6.1). So the gap is underspecification + turn-splitting, not
   information lost in sharding. Weak models pay a rephrasing tax: Llama3.1-8B, OLMo-2-13B and
   **Claude 3 Haiku** reach only **86–92 %** of Full on Concat; strong models are near parity.
3. **Frontier models get lost as badly as small ones.** "More performant models (**Claude 3.7
   Sonnet**, Gemini 2.5, GPT-4.1) get equally lost in conversation compared to smaller models
   (Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, Phi-4)", all at **30–40 % average degradation** (§6.1). The only other
   Claude-specific statement in the paper: **Claude 3.7 Sonnet and GPT-4.1 "conserve performance well
   on Code"** — per-task numbers are in the table that did not survive conversion. Reasoning models
   (o3, R1) degrade like the rest: extra test-time compute buys no multi-turn strategy, and their
   responses run **33 % longer**, which the authors tie to the verbosity result (6d).
4. **Decomposition: small aptitude loss, huge reliability loss.** Full→Sharded, aptitude (`A⁹⁰`,
   p90 over N=10 runs) drops **16 %** — the authors call this non-significant — while unreliability
   (`U⁹⁰₁₀`, p90−p10) **rises 112 %, more than doubling**. In Sharded, every model converges on
   **~50 points between best and worst run on a fixed instruction**, regardless of aptitude (§6.2).
   Single-turn keeps the usual higher-aptitude→more-reliable relationship; multi-turn destroys it.
5. **Two turns is enough to trigger it.** Gradual sharding (31 instructions re-sharded into 2–8
   shards at fixed complexity; GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini only): the dip appears at **2 shards** and
   barely worsens with granularity. "Providing all the information at once (1-shard) is the only
   effective method to improve reliability" (§6.3).
6. **Four mechanisms (Appendix 15, all observational).**
   a. *Premature answer attempts* — conversations whose first answer attempt falls in the first 20 %
   of turns score **30.9**; those where it falls in the last 20 % score **64.4**, ordering holding for
   every model. Measurable only on Code and Math; elsewhere models answer from turn 1 nearly always.
   b. *Answer bloat* — final Sharded answers run **20–300 % longer** than Full/Concat for the same
   instruction, growing with each attempt. Even *correct* Sharded solutions bloat: Code **850 vs 668
   chars (+27 %)**, SQL **129 vs 113 (+14 %)**. The model accretes on its earlier assumptions instead
   of invalidating them.
   c. *Loss-in-middle-turns* — on Summary (docs introduced turn by turn, citations traceable):
   turn-1 summaries cite turn-1 docs 96 %; turn-2 splits 48/49; by turn 8, **20 % of citations go to
   turn-8 docs vs 8 % for turns 2–3**. Lost-in-the-middle, over turns rather than context position.
   Averaged over all 15 models; "more muted" in stronger models, no per-model numbers given.
   d. *Over-verbosity* — binning the N=10 runs per (model, instruction) by mean per-turn response
   length, **the shortest quintile beats the longest by 10–50 % on 5 of 6 tasks**. Actions is the
   exception, where terseness hurt.
7. **What recovers the loss, and how much** (§7.1, App. 22; 4 tasks, GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini only).
   **Recap** — one final turn restating all prior user turns — scores best but the authors call it
   unrealistic, since it requires knowing which turn is last. **Snowball** — every turn restates all
   shards so far — **mitigates 15–20 % of the Full→Sharded deterioration**. Both "still lag behind
   Full or Concat". The authors' conclusion: re-injecting prior turns from an agent framework is a
   partial patch, not a fix.
8. **Temperature is not the lever.** In Full/Concat, T 1.0→0.0 cuts unreliability **50–80 %**. In
   Sharded, GPT-4o-mini improves not at all and GPT-4o only **15–20 %**; **~30 % unreliability
   remains with user and assistant both at T=0** (§7.2). One early-token divergence cascades.
9. **Episodic multi-turn tasks do not show the effect.** A seventh task (Translation, each turn adds
   two sentences) shows **no Sharded degradation** (BLEU within 10 %). The authors' stated
   preconditions for getting lost: generative (not extractive/classification), complex enough to
   yield several shards, and **non-decomposable — a new shard changes the whole solution** rather
   than appending to it (§7.3).

## Method and setting

Single-turn benchmark instructions are sharded semi-automatically (GPT-4o proposes, authors review;
~3 h per 100 instructions) into minimal shards, shard 1 always carrying the high-level intent. A
**GPT-4o-mini user simulator** holding the whole sharded instruction then reveals at most one shard
per turn, rephrasing it to fit; a GPT-4o-mini classifier labels each assistant turn (answer attempt /
clarification / interrogation / discussion / hedging / refusal / missing) and answer attempts are
extracted and scored. The assistant is **never told** it is in a multi-turn underspecified setting.

Six tasks, 600 instructions: **Code** (HumanEval + LiveCodeBench, execution-scored), **Database**
(Spider text-to-SQL), **Actions** (BFCL function calling), **Math** (GSM8K), **Data-to-text** (ToTTo,
BLEU), **Summary** (Summary-of-a-Haystack mini-haystacks, 20 docs, LLM judge). 15 models across 8
families — including **Claude 3 Haiku and Claude 3.7 Sonnet via Amazon Bedrock**, GPT-4o-mini/4o/
4.1/o3, Gemini 2.5 Flash/Pro, three Llamas, OLMo-2-13B, Phi-4, DeepSeek-R1, Command-A. **T=1.0, max
response 1,000 tokens** (10,000 for o3/R1), N=10 per (model, instruction, setting), >200,000
conversations, ~$5,000. Nothing is trained; everything is prompting; code and data released. A
200-conversation hand audit found simulator errors in <5 % of conversations, disfavouring the
assistant in <2 %. The authors argue the simulation is *benign* — information always sufficient by
the end, no derailing, no frustration — so the degradation measured is likely an **underestimate**.

## Relevance to P1–P4

**P2 (sessions grow; the longest dominate).** *Loss-in-middle-turns* is the only direct evidence:
within one long interaction, material introduced in the middle is systematically under-weighted
(turn 8: 20 % of citations to the newest turn vs 8 % to turns 2–3), and the artefact bloats
monotonically with each iteration. Our doc-writer (192 turns, up to 438k, one session over the whole
diff) and long code-writer phases have the shape the paper penalises. But the paper never measured
tool-loop turns — where the *model* pulls the information — and never a 100k+ token session, so this
is a hypothesis to test, not a result to cite.

**P3 (every session rebuilds the same picture).** The paper cuts *against* the obvious economy. Its
central positive result is that consolidating everything into the first turn is what works (Concat =
95.1 % of Full), and partial restatement recovers only 15–20 %. It never varies how much context a
session has — only *when* the context arrives. A "read less, re-derive less" intervention justified
by this paper would be a misread; the supported move is "arrive complete, once".

**P4 (we do not know what smaller reads cost in grounding).** The paper hands us a measurement method
rather than an answer: run the identical unit N times and report **A⁹⁰** and **U⁹⁰₁₀** separately,
because a mean-quality change can be a capability loss or a variance explosion and the two need
different fixes. Their −16 % aptitude / +112 % unreliability split is exactly the decomposition our
blocking-finding-rate and gate-red instruments collapse into one number. It also warns that a
single-run A/B on curated-vs-full context is uninterpretable: in the multi-turn regime, best-vs-worst
run on a *fixed* input spans ~50 points.

**P1 (context is the cost).** No bearing — the paper measures task success, never tokens or spend.

## Interventions this paper supports

- **Make every dispatch prompt a Concat, not a conversation.** A dispatch omitting a constraint the
  agent later needs puts the session in the Sharded regime. *Rests on:* Concat 95.1 % of Full vs
  Sharded −39 %, and 2 shards sufficing (§6.1, §6.3). *Loses:* nothing the paper measures — but the
  Concat rephrasing tax (86–92 % for weak models) says a *summarised* restatement is not free; carry
  the plan's and the operator's wording verbatim rather than paraphrasing it into the prompt.
- **Relay operator clarifications by restarting, not by appending a turn.** An exit-4 answer fed back
  into a session that has already produced an implementation *is* the sharded second turn. *Rests
  on:* premature-answer (30.9 vs 64.4), answer bloat (+27 % even when correct), and the authors'
  explicit user advice — "if time allows, try again" / "consolidate before retrying" (§7.4). *Loses:*
  the restart re-pays orientation reads (P2's 14–65 turns, ~38 % of session cost); the paper offers
  no way to have both.
- **Fix rounds must carry the consolidated spec, not just the finding.** A writer handed a bare
  "blocking finding X" is in the same regime; the fix dispatch should restate phase outcome + ACs +
  finding as one complete instruction. *Rests on:* Snowball's 15–20 % recovery. *Loses:* prompt
  tokens, and Snowball's own evidence that restatement never reaches Full.
- **Keep fresh-session-per-role; the paper is evidence for it.** "Starting a new conversation that
  repeats the same information might yield significantly better outcomes than continuing an ongoing
  conversation" (§7.4), citing Cursor users' new-chat-often practice. The condition attached: the new
  session must receive the *consolidated* instruction. It follows that a hand-off artefact shaped as
  a *delta since last session* is wrong; a self-contained restatement is right.
- **Bound sub-agent return contracts to short, decision-shaped answers.** *Rests on:* the verbosity
  quintiles (shortest beats longest 10–50 % on 5/6 tasks) plus the authors' hypothesis that long
  turns inject assumptions later turns must fight. *Size:* unquantifiable for us — correlational, and
  their responses were capped at 1,000 tokens. *Loses:* grounding the caller needed; Actions, the
  most tool-shaped of the six tasks, is the paper's own counter-example where terseness hurt.
- **Instrument with repeats and report spread, not mean.** *Rests on:* §6.2's method. Cheapest
  version: re-run one representative phase N times against a fixed plan, report p90/p10 of blocking
  findings and gate-red. The only thing here that directly attacks P4.

## Applicability caveats

- **Claude coverage is thin and stale.** Two Claude models, both older than ours, default settings,
  no extended thinking. Beyond "30–40 % degradation" and "conserves performance well on Code" for
  3.7 Sonnet, and Haiku's 86–92 % Concat, **no Claude number is recoverable from this file** — the
  per-model table was lost in conversion. Nothing about Opus, nothing about maximum effort.
- **Task type is half-right.** Code and Database are our shape; Math, Data-to-text and Summary are
  not. Even Code is single-function HumanEval/LiveCodeBench scale, test-executed, under a
  **1,000-token response cap**, with **no tools, no repository, no file reads, no execution
  feedback**. Our agents' turns are mostly self-directed tool calls, not an external party revealing
  withheld requirements — the paper never studied that regime, and the distinction is the crux, since
  its mechanism is about information the model did not have *and could not fetch*.
- **Idealised in both directions.** Minimal shards, one per turn, is more adversarial than reality;
  guaranteed-sufficient-by-the-end with no derailing is more benign. The authors judge the net an
  underestimate.
- **Horizon is short.** Conversations run a handful of turns (5–10 shards; 8-turn Summary analyses)
  against our 20–200. Extrapolating loss-in-middle-turns from 8 turns to 192 is unwarranted here.
- **Two mechanism findings are correlational** (answer-attempt timing, verbosity): the paper bins
  observed conversations, it never instructs a model to delay answering or to be terse and measures
  the result. Interventions built on them are untested even in the paper's own setting.
- **Nothing needs training** — the transfer barrier is setting, not method.

## Briefing check

The note — "bears on dispatch-prompt completeness" — is **supported, and it is the paper's strongest
claim**. The result that does it: Concat, the sharded information delivered *complete in the first
turn*, retains **95.1 % of Full**, while the same information split across turns loses **39 %**; and
gradual sharding shows **two shards already suffice** — "providing all the information at once
(1-shard) is the only effective method to improve reliability" (§6.3). A dispatch prompt missing one
requirement that later has to be supplied is structurally a 2-shard instruction.

Two refinements the note does not carry. First, the evidence is about *when* information arrives, not
*how much* there is — it lends no support to trimming what a session reads, and would be misapplied
that way (a live risk, since P3 and P4 invite exactly that reading). Second, the more actionable half
for us may not be the dispatch prompt but the **within-loop increments**: exit-4 answers relayed into
a running session, and blocking findings returned to a writer that has already implemented. Those are
literal sharded turns, and the paper's remedy for them recovers only **15–20 %** — which argues for
restarting with a consolidated brief rather than continuing. Nothing in the note is overstated.
