## Core results

1. **Capability saturation is the paper's single robust finding.** Coordination yields negative
   returns once the single-agent baseline exceeds ≈45% success. Interaction
   `P_SA × log(1+n_a)`: β̂ = −0.236, p = 0.004 (§1, §4.3). Decision boundary
   P*_SA = β̂₄/β̂₁₇ = 0.040/0.236 = 0.170 standardized ≈ **0.45 raw**. `single_agent_baseline` is
   the only predictor surviving *both* cluster-robust inference (p = 0.004, Table 8) and
   Holm–Bonferroni over 19 hypotheses (p_Holm = 0.018, Table 9). Threshold match rate 94% across
   the 16 model × benchmark cells of SWE-bench + Terminal-Bench (binomial p < 0.001).

2. **Per-architecture results (Table 3, N = 260, all six benchmarks).** Measured, not conjectured:

   | Metric | SAS | Independent | Decentralized | Centralized | Hybrid |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | Success rate | 0.466 | 0.370 | 0.477 | 0.463 | 0.452 |
   | Turns | 7.2 ± 2.1 | 11.4 ± 3.2 | 26.1 ± 7.5 | 27.7 ± 8.1 | 44.3 ± 12.4 |
   | Overhead O% | 0 | 58 | 263 | 285 | 515 |
   | Message density c | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.41 | 0.39 | 0.24 |
   | Redundancy R | 0.00 | 0.48 | 0.50 | 0.41 | 0.46 |
   | Efficiency E_c | 0.466 | 0.234 | 0.132 | 0.120 | 0.074 |
   | Trace error amp A_e | 1.0 | **17.2** [14.3, 20.1] | 7.8 | **4.4** [3.8, 5.0] | 5.1 |
   | Success / 1K tokens | **67.7** | 42.4 | 23.9 | 21.5 | **13.6** |

   Mean MAS improvement over SAS across all six benchmarks is **−0.3%** (95% CI [−58.7%, +77.2%],
   σ = 37.5%) — i.e. no average benefit, enormous heterogeneity.

3. **Per-benchmark results (§4.2), relative to SAS.** Finance Agent (D = 0.407, 5 tools, decomposable):
   Centralized **+80.8%** (0.631 vs 0.349), Decentralized +74.5%, Hybrid +73.1%. BrowseComp-Plus
   (D = 0.839): Decentralized +9.2% (0.347 vs 0.318), Centralized +0.2%, Independent −35%.
   Workbench (D = 0.000, **16 tools**): Decentralized +5.6% (0.664 vs 0.629), Centralized/Hybrid
   −1.2%. PlanCraft (D = 0.419, sequential): **all** MAS degrade — Hybrid −39.1%, Decentralized
   −41.5%, Centralized −50.3%, Independent −70.0%. **SWE-bench Verified (D = 0.255, 7 tools): all
   MAS degrade — Hybrid −2.1% (0.511), Centralized −3.1% (0.506), Decentralized −5.4% (0.494),
   Independent −14.9% (0.444), vs SAS 0.522.** Terminal-Bench (2 tools, SAS 0.344): Independent
   +1.7%, Centralized −19.2%.

4. **Predictors and effect sizes (standardized; Eq. eq:scaling_law, 20 params).**
   - `single_agent_baseline` / capability ceiling: β̂ = −0.236, p = 0.004 (robust: survives).
   - `intelligence_centered`: β̂_I = +0.126, p = 0.008; quadratic I² p = 0.977 (linear, not emergent).
   - `efficiency_x_tools` (E_c × T), the **tool-coordination trade-off**: β̂ = −0.096,
     95% CI [−0.154, −0.037], p = 0.002; **cluster-robust p = 0.205 (does not survive)**;
     Holm p = 0.026. Authors call it "a consistent directional pattern", not a confirmed effect.
   - `log_tools`: p < 0.001 naive, Holm < 0.001, **robust p = 0.172 (inflated)**.
   - `O% × T`: β̂ = −0.033, p = 0.211 on six benchmarks (β̂ = −0.162, p < 0.001 on the 4-benchmark subset).
   - `A_e^trace` main effect: β̂ = 0.014, p = 0.658; `A_e × T`: β̂ = 0.022, p = 0.332 — **error
     amplification is NOT significant once efficiency and overhead are controlled** (§4.3).
   - `R × n_a`: β̂ = +0.024, CI [0.002, 0.047], p = 0.034 (4× smaller than the efficiency penalty).
   - Fit: R²_train = 0.463, **R²_CV = 0.373 ± 0.170** (Intelligence Index); ACI variant 0.481 /
     **0.413 ± 0.130**, AIC −244.8 (Table 7). Architecture selection on held-out configurations:
     **87%** correct vs 20% random, 54% capability-only.

5. **Turn/coordination cost scales super-linearly with agent count.** T = 2.72 (n + 0.5)^1.724,
   R² = 0.974, exponent CI [1.685, 1.763], p < 0.001. Hybrid needs 6.2× SAS turns
   (t(178) = 16.8, p < 0.001), Centralized 3.8×, Decentralized 3.6×. Extrapolated: ≈69 turns at
   n = 6, ≈157 turns at n = 10 (9.5×–21.8× SAS). Authors state a "hard resource ceiling ... beyond
   3–4 agents". Token multipliers 1.6–6.2× vs SAS. Three regimes (§4.4): under-coordination
   (O < 100%, ΔS ≈ +2–4%), **optimal band (200% < O < 300%, E_c ≈ 0.16)**, over-coordination
   (O > 400%, E_c ≈ 0.11).

6. **Failure classes and rates (§4.4, MAST-derived).**
   - *Logical contradiction*: baseline 12.3–18.7% → Centralized 9.1% (−36.4%), Decentralized 11.5%,
     Independent 16.8% (unchanged).
   - *Numerical drift*: baseline 20.9–24.1% → Cent/Dec 18.3% (−24%), **Hybrid amplifies to 26.4%**,
     Independent 23.2%.
   - *Context omission* (failure to reference previously established state — closest to our P3):
     baseline 15.8–25.2% → **Centralized 8.3% (−66.8%) via orchestrator synthesis**, Decentralized
     11.2%, Independent 24.1% (unchanged).
   - *Coordination failure* (MAS-only): Independent 0%, Centralized 1.8%, Decentralized 3.2%,
     **Hybrid 12.4%**.
   - Error absorption (E_SAS − E_MAS)/E_SAS: **22.7%** mean (CI [20.1%, 25.3%]), peak 31.4% on
     Finance; Independent shows **+4.6% amplification** (no verification mechanism).
   - Task-level A_e^task ≈ 1.1–1.3 vs trace-level 4–17× — the 17.2× is *extra computational work*
     from coordination failure, not 17× more wrong answers. Do not conflate.

7. **Message/redundancy saturation.** S = 0.73 + 0.28 ln(c), R² = 0.68, p < 0.001, plateau at
   c* = 0.39 messages/turn. Beyond it messages add redundancy, not information (Hybrid vs
   Centralized: −2.4%, n.s., t(178) = 0.61, p = 0.542). High redundancy hurts: R > 0.50 correlates
   negatively with success (r = −0.136, p = 0.004); optimum R ≈ 0.41. Successful runs show
   contradictory-token mass median 2.3% vs 8.1% in failures.

8. **Anthropic-specific claims (all §4.2/§4.4 and figure captions; see Briefing check for quotes).**
   Anthropic Hybrid costs **≈$0.024 per 1% success gain vs OpenAI $0.008 (3× worse) and Google
   $0.012**. On PlanCraft Anthropic's worst degradation is **−54.5%** (Hybrid 0.31 vs SAS 0.68) vs
   Google −25.3%, OpenAI −32.3%. But counter-evidence in the same section: on Workbench (16 tools)
   **Anthropic's best variant is the best of the three families (+10.8% vs Google +9.5%, OpenAI
   +8.6%), "reflecting relative efficiency in managing coordination costs"**; on Finance Anthropic
   Centralized is +127.5% (0.636 vs 0.280). Anthropic Centralized has the *lowest cross-task
   variance* of any family (mean 43%, SD 2.3%). Crucially the paper also reports that
   architecture-specific **scaling slopes are near-identical across families** (Δ_max = 0.023,
   CV < 0.02), and explicitly labels family differences "empirical signatures rather than
   mechanistic conclusions".

9. **Sub-agent capability matters more than orchestrator capability** (Fig. 4, BrowseComp-Plus):
   "in centralized architectures, configurations with high-capability sub-agents outperform those
   with high-capability orchestrators across all model families." Anthropic uniquely: weak
   orchestrator + strong sub-agents 0.42 beats homogeneous strong 0.32 (+31%). But heterogeneous
   *centralized* configs underperform the strong homogeneous baseline by 12.6 pp on average
   (Table 6); e.g. Sonnet-3.7 orch + Sonnet-4.5 sub = 0.42 (−0.01), Sonnet-4.5 orch + 3.7 sub = 0.37
   (−0.06).

## Method and setting

260 configurations = 5 architectures × 9 models (8 on SWE-bench/Terminal-Bench, Sonnet 3.7 retired)
× 6 benchmarks. Models: GPT-5-nano/mini/5; Gemini 2.0-Flash/2.5-Flash/2.5-Pro; **Claude Sonnet
3.7/4/4.5 — no Opus, no extended thinking, no agentic harness**. Intelligence Index 42–71.
Architectures: SAS (one loop, max **10 iterations**); Independent (3 agents → `synthesis_only`
concatenating aggregator, no cross-validation); Centralized (1 orchestrator + 3 sub-agents, ≤5
rounds × 3 iterations); Decentralized (3 agents × 3 debate rounds × 3 iterations); Hybrid
(orchestrator + peer edges). Benchmarks/tools: Workbench 16, SWE-bench Verified 7, Finance 5,
PlanCraft 4, Terminal-Bench 2. Instance counts 50–100, except **SWE-bench and Terminal-Bench = 20
instances each** (bootstrap 95% CIs ≈ ±20 pp per cell, Table 10). Implementation: LiteLLM +
LangChain, frozen weights, **nothing trained**; identical prompts across architectures, *not*
per-model optimized (limitation iv). Horizon is short: mean **4,800 reasoning tokens per trial**,
7.2–44.3 turns; "context window management automatically truncates conversation history when token
limits are approached" — i.e. compaction is on, unlike ours. Code is open
(github.com/ybkim95/agent-scaling) with per-instance results.

Claude-specific SWE-bench cells (Table 10, 20 instances, ±20 pp): Sonnet-4 SAS **70** vs Centralized
50, Decentralized 55, Hybrid 45, Independent 30; Sonnet-4.5 SAS **75** vs Cent 70, Dec 70, Hyb 70,
Ind 55. Terminal-Bench: Sonnet-4 SAS 25 / Cent 25 / Dec 35 / Hyb 25 / Ind 35; Sonnet-4.5 SAS 60 /
Cent 45 / Dec 40 / Hyb 50 / Ind 40.

## Relevance to P1–P4

**P1 (context is the cost).** Direct and quantitative: coordination costs 1.6–6.2× the token budget
and 2–6× the efficiency (E_c 0.466 → 0.074); success/1K tokens falls 67.7 → 21.5 (Centralized) →
13.6 (Hybrid). Every sub-agent we add is priced at that scale. Transfer gap: the paper measures
*reasoning tokens at fixed budget*, has **no model of prompt caching**, no cache-read/write split,
and no per-session fixed prefix — our 33k prefix × 30 sessions and the 63%-cache-read structure are
outside its accounting entirely. Internal inconsistency worth flagging: Table 3's caption says "all
systems matched for total reasoning tokens (mean μ = 4,800 per trial)" while the same table reports
O% = 58–515% and a 5× spread in success/1K tokens.

**P2 (sessions grow; the longest dominate).** Only indirectly. The growth law is over *agent count*
(T = 2.72(n+0.5)^1.724), not session length; the longest configuration is 44 turns and ~5k tokens,
two to three orders of magnitude below our 80k–438k contexts and 20–200 turns. Its one transferable
warning is the "hard resource ceiling beyond 3–4 agents" under a fixed budget — which we do not
operate under, since our sub-agents are *additive* compute, not a split of a fixed pot.

**P3 (every session rebuilds the same picture).** The paper's central mechanism: SAS "maximizes
context integration"; MAS "imposes intrinsic information fragmentation ... the global context must
be compressed into inter-agent messages", the step it names the *coordination tax*. It gives P3 two
instruments we lack: the **Context Omission** error class (15.8–25.2% baseline → 8.3% under
orchestrator synthesis, 24.1% under no verification) and **redundancy R** = fraction of subtasks
performed by more than one agent, with a measured optimum at R ≈ 0.41 and a penalty above 0.50
(r = −0.136, p = 0.004). Our per-session re-reads are the R > 0.5 regime.

**P4 (unmeasured grounding cost of smaller reads).** The paper does not vary context curation — it
varies topology — so it cannot answer P4. What it contributes is a metric set that is cheap for us
to compute from `state.json` and turn counts (E_c = S/(T/T_SAS), O%, R, coordination-failure rate,
A_e^task = E_MAS/E_SAS) and a validated claim that **error propagation is *not* the mechanism**
(A_e main effect β̂ = 0.014, p = 0.658) — efficiency and overhead are. If we instrument one thing,
instrument efficiency, not error cascades.

## Interventions this paper supports

- **Gate fan-out on the parent's own success baseline, not on task size.** Capability saturation
  (β̂ = −0.236, p = 0.004, the only doubly-robust finding; 94% threshold match) says a sub-agent is
  a net loss where the single-agent baseline already exceeds ~45%. Our code-writer phases run
  against a deterministic green gate — a high-baseline regime, where the paper predicts tax and no
  gain. Avoids a 2–6× efficiency loss for ≈0 accuracy. *Loses:* nothing measured; the paper never
  tests curated hand-offs.
- **Never use an Independent (concatenating) aggregation of sub-agent outputs.** Independent is the
  only architecture that underperforms SAS *universally* (0.370 vs 0.466), amplifies trace errors
  17.2× and corrects nothing (+4.6% error amplification; Context Omission unchanged at 24.1%). Any
  fan-out must land on a parent that *verifies* before merging: Centralized-style verification cuts
  Context Omission 66.8% and Logical Contradiction 36.4%.
- **Keep exactly one coordination layer; never orchestrator-plus-peer.** Hybrid is worst on every
  cost axis (515% overhead, E_c 0.074, 13.6 successes/1K tokens, **12.4% coordination-failure
  rate**, numerical drift *amplified* to 26.4%), costs Anthropic models $0.024 per 1% gain, and is
  the architecture the fitted model over-predicts most (+37.0% mean error out-of-sample).
- **Cap sub-agents at 3 per dispatching session.** T = 2.72(n+0.5)^1.724 — 69 turns at n = 6, 157 at
  n = 10 — and the authors' explicit "hard resource ceiling beyond 3–4 agents".
- **Make the sub-agent return contract a *converged conclusion*, and bound how much comes back.**
  S = 0.73 + 0.28 ln(c) saturates at c* = 0.39 messages/turn; past it messages add redundancy, not
  information. Successful runs have contradictory-token mass 2.3% (median) vs 8.1% in failures —
  contradiction density is a cheap accept/reject signal for a sub-agent's return.
- **Target redundancy ≈ 0.41, not 0.** Duplication's measured benefit is small but real
  (β̂_{R×n_a} = +0.024, p = 0.034); above R = 0.50 it turns negative. Some re-reading by a sub-agent
  is protective; wholesale re-derivation of the parent's picture is the failure mode. *Loses:* the
  paper gives no method for hitting a target R, only the correlation.
- **Do not weaken sub-agents to save cost.** "Configurations with high-capability sub-agents
  outperform those with high-capability orchestrators across all model families"; heterogeneous
  centralized configs still lose 12.6 pp to strong-homogeneous. Evidence *for* the settled
  no-weaker-model rule, not a re-opening.
- **Instrument E_c and O%, not error cascades** (see P4 above).

## Applicability caveats

- **Models.** Claude Sonnet 3.7/4/4.5 only — no Opus, no extended thinking/effort, no Claude Code
  harness, prompts not tuned per family (limitation iv). Every "Anthropic" number is a Sonnet number.
- **Horizon.** ~4,800 reasoning tokens and 7.2–44.3 turns per trial, with truncation enabled. Our
  sessions are 80k–438k tokens over 20–200 turns with no compaction. The paper's regime is closer to
  one of our tool-loop turns than to one of our sessions.
- **Matched-budget design is the biggest transfer gap.** MAS agents receive *smaller per-agent*
  iteration budgets so total compute matches SAS. PlanCraft's −70% is partly budget-splitting, not
  coordination per se. Our sub-agents are additive compute, so the *degradation* numbers overstate
  our risk while the *token multipliers* understate nothing.
- **Coding evidence is the thinnest cell in the paper.** SWE-bench Verified = 20 instances, bootstrap
  CIs ±20 pp, so no individual Claude comparison (e.g. Sonnet-4 SAS 70 vs Centralized 50) is
  statistically separable. Aggregate direction (all MAS ≤ SAS) is what holds.
- **Table 3's coordination metrics are "architecture-level constants ... applied uniformly across
  all benchmarks."** E_c, A_e, R and c are therefore near-proxies for the architecture label inside
  the regression — the R²_CV = 0.373 is not evidence that per-task coordination measurement predicts
  performance.
- **Statistical fragility.** With G = 6 clusters, only `single_agent_baseline` and `error_x_baseline`
  survive cluster-robust inference; `log_tools`, `efficiency_x_tools` and `baseline_x_agents` inflate
  up to 2.9× and are reported as directional. Leave-one-dataset-out generalization is explicitly
  described as poor for absolute prediction; the 87% figure is *within-domain* architecture ranking.
- **Nothing is trained**, so every intervention is prompt/topology-level — the same surface we have.

## Briefing check

The note — *"S4 finds tool-heavy and sequential tasks pay a coordination tax, Anthropic models most
sensitive; 'sub-agents everywhere' is not supported as framed"* — is **right in direction, partly
overstated in strength**.

*Supported.* Sequential tasks: "PlanCraft exhibits universal performance degradation across all
multi-agent architectures" (−39.1% to −70.0%), and "sequential interdependence, rather than
complexity alone, determines coordination viability" (Finance D = 0.41 → +80.8% vs PlanCraft
D = 0.42 → −70%). "Sub-agents everywhere" is squarely refuted: mean MAS improvement is **−0.3%
(95% CI [−58.7%, +77.2%])**, and on the coding benchmark **all** MAS variants lose to SAS.

*Overstated — tool-heaviness.* The tool-coordination trade-off (β̂_{E_c×T} = −0.096, p = 0.002) does
**not survive cluster-robust inference (p = 0.205)**; the authors head that subsection "Emerges as a
Consistent Directional Pattern" and say it "should not be interpreted by directly multiplying raw
values". It also does not mean tool-heavy ⇒ single-agent: on the most tool-heavy benchmark
(Workbench, 16 tools) Decentralized *wins* (+5.6%, 0.664) and the paper's own selection rule says
"*Tool-heavy tasks* (T = 16, P_SA = 0.63) favor decentralized multi-agent despite high overhead
(263%)". The correct statement is: tool-rich environments penalize *high-overhead* architectures
(Hybrid collapses to 0.21 on Workbench), not all coordination.

*Overstated — "Anthropic most sensitive".* The exact supporting quotes are: "Anthropic models reveal
higher variance and occasional MAS underperformance, **reflecting sensitivity to coordination
overhead**" (Fig. 3 caption) and "Anthropic Hybrid reaches ≈$0.024 per 1% gain (3× worse,
**reflecting Anthropic's sensitivity to coordination overhead**)" (§4.4). Both are cost/variance
claims about Hybrid, sourced to figure captions and a marginal-cost ratio, not to a tested
interaction. Against them, the same paper reports Anthropic *best of three families* on the
tool-heaviest benchmark (+10.8% Decentralized vs Google +9.5%, OpenAI +8.6%, "reflecting relative
efficiency in managing coordination costs"), Anthropic Centralized as the *lowest-variance* family
(mean 43%, SD 2.3%), Anthropic Centralized +127.5% on Finance, and — decisively — that
architecture-specific scaling slopes differ across families by at most **Δ_max = 0.023 (CV < 0.02)**,
with the authors cautioning these are "empirical signatures rather than mechanistic conclusions".
Anthropic-sensitivity should be carried as a *dollar-cost* caveat for high-overhead topologies, not
as a model-family law.

**Transfer answer for our three candidate uses.** (a) *Orientation-only exploration returning a
conclusion to a writer* — this is Centralized with n = 1 and a verifying parent, the paper's
safest shape (A_e 4.4× not 17.2×, Context Omission 8.3%), and it is supported **only where the
parent's baseline is below ~45%**: plan-writer/plan-reviewer orientation on an unfamiliar surface,
yes; a code-writer whose phase is gate-verified, no. (b) *Per-surface doc sub-agents with a
consistency pass* — the best-supported of the three: it is the Finance-Agent shape (independently
analysable sub-streams + orchestrator synthesis, +80.8%), the consistency pass is precisely the
validation bottleneck that takes Context Omission from 24.1% to 8.3%, and the doc-writer's
438k-token single session is the fragmentation-free extreme the paper prices at 3.1× worse
success-per-token — with the caveat that decomposability must be real, since PlanCraft shows
artificial decomposition of a sequential task costing −50%. (c) *Evidence gathering for a reviewer
returning file:line citations* — the one the paper predicts pays a tax: it is code, tool-heavy, and
run against a reviewer whose baseline is high; SWE-bench Verified shows every MAS variant below SAS
(−2.1% to −14.9%), with Claude Sonnet-4 at SAS 70 vs Centralized 50 (±20 pp, underpowered). Keep it
only if the citations are *verified by the reviewer* rather than trusted, and expect cost, not
accuracy, to be the deciding term.
