## Core results

1. **Horizon length grows hyperbolically in per-step accuracy** (Proposition 1, §Formulation;
   proof in App. "Proof and Analysis of Proposition 1"). Assuming constant independent step accuracy
   `p` and no self-correction, `H_s(p) = ⌈ln s / ln p⌉`; at `s=0.5`, `H_0.5 ≈ ln2/(1−p)` in the
   near-perfect regime, and `dH_0.5/dp ≈ ln2/(1−p)²` — *quadratic* return on step accuracy as `p→1`.
   Consequence the authors stress: METR's observed doubling of software-engineering horizon length
   every 7 months only requires step accuracy `2^(−1/2^t)`, i.e. exponential horizon growth is
   consistent with visibly *diminishing* single-step gains. Growth is sharp beyond ≈80 % step
   accuracy. This is analysis, not measurement.

2. **Execution alone fails long before knowledge does** (Result 1, Fig. 3a/3c). With plan and
   knowledge supplied in context, every model except Gemma3-4B / Qwen3-4B has near-perfect *first
   step* accuracy, yet the best open model tested (Qwen3-32B) drops below 50 % task accuracy
   **within 15 turns** at turn complexity K=1. Measured.

3. **Model size buys turns, non-diminishingly** (Result 2, Fig. 3b–d). Horizon length rises clearly
   and (over 4 sizes) non-diminishingly with parameter count, on a task that is neither
   knowledge- nor complexity-constrained. Authors decline to fit a scaling law (≤4 sizes per family).
   Conjectured mechanism: circuit redundancy ensembling away error — and they show output-level
   parallel aggregation does *not* reproduce it (App. "Can Parallel Test-time Compute Scaling Match
   Thinking?").

4. **Per-turn accuracy decays, and part of the decay is self-conditioning** (Result 3, Fig. 4a/4b).
   Counterfactual: inject synthetic assistant history at a chosen error rate, then measure turn-100
   accuracy. At induced error rate 0.00 ("healed" history) turn-100 accuracy is already below turn-1
   — that part is long-context degradation. As the injected error rate rises, turn-100 accuracy
   **degrades further and monotonically** — that part is self-conditioning: the model's own errors in
   context make later errors likelier. Effect sizes are only shown in figures (not numerically in
   text), so only the direction and monotonicity are quotable.

5. **Scale fixes long-context, not self-conditioning** (Result 4, Fig. 4b). Frontier *non-thinking*
   models — Kimi-K2 (1026B), DeepSeek-V3 (670B), Qwen3-235B-Instruct-2507 — reach near-perfect
   turn-100 accuracy **on a healed history**, i.e. 100-turn long-context degradation is essentially
   solved at that scale. All of them still degrade monotonically as induced error rate rises. The
   Fig. 4 caption goes further than the body text: "Scaling model size **increases**
   self-conditioning, even for frontier non-thinking models." Authors link it to in-context learning
   (larger models are better at continuing their context) and to hallucination snowballing.

6. **Thinking fixes self-conditioning** (Result 5, Fig. "thinking-selfcond"). Qwen3 *thinking*
   models show turn-100 accuracy **flat with respect to the induced error rate**. Two candidate
   causes, both stated as conjecture: (a) RL training orients the model to task success rather than
   context continuation; (b) prior-turn thinking traces are *removed from history* (see Method), so
   each turn is reasoned about independently — inspection of traces shows the models "do not refer
   back to their answers in prior turns." CoT prompting alone does **not** fix it: with
   programmatically generated flawed CoT histories, Gemma3 still self-conditions (App.
   "Chain-of-Thought Self-Conditioning", Fig. 19).

7. **Steps per turn without thinking is tiny** (Result 6, Fig. 5 left). Answering directly, Qwen3-32B,
   Gemma3-27B, DeepSeek-V3 (670B) and Kimi-K2 (1026B) all fail beyond a turn complexity of **six**
   (abstract: DeepSeek-V3 "fail[s] at performing even four steps"; its thinking version R1 executes
   "over 100"). On the harder 2×2 matrix-multiplication variant (App. "Sequentiality"), non-thinking
   models cannot chain even **two** matrices; thinking raises it substantially.

8. **Frontier single-turn horizon benchmark** (Result 7, Fig. 5 right). Binary search for the largest
   K executed at ≥80 % accuracy: **GPT-5 thinking ("Horizon") 2176 steps; Claude-4 Sonnet 432;
   Grok 4 384; Gemini 2.5 Pro 120.** Qwen3-Next (hybrid Gated DeltaNet + attention) beats larger
   pure-attention models — architecture matters. 20–50 rollouts per frontier model.

9. **Self-conditioning is visible in real agent traces** (App. "Self-Conditioning in Realistic
   Agentic Tasks"). Manual re-annotation of AgentErrorBench failed trajectories (GPT-4o):
   ≈**20 % of GAIA, 48 % of ALFWorld, 33 % of WebShop** failures match self-conditioning patterns
   (`inefficient_plan`, `progress_misjudge`, `causal_misattribution`). Authors flag this as
   subjective, hence the synthetic study.

10. **Context engineering works; self-verification and majority voting do not** (App. "Investigating
    Potential Fixes"). A sliding window keeping only the N most recent turns improves performance
    "significantly as the context window size is reduced", sustaining longer horizons (Gemma3-12B,
    Fig. 10a) — but relies on the task being Markovian. Prompted self-verification gives Gemma3+CoT
    an early boost, then *worse* collapse because verification tokens exhaust the context sooner;
    Qwen3 thinking models overthink and make arithmetic errors inside the verification itself.
    Majority voting matched for token budget against CoT gives only marginal gains — sequential
    compute beats parallel here, unlike math/commonsense.

11. **State management, not the atomic operations, is the error source** (App. "Deconstructing
    Errors"). Stateless retrieval-only and addition-only tasks are near-perfect over long horizons;
    the stateful prefix-sum task degrades slowly; the full retrieve-then-compose task degrades most.

12. **No universal optimum for steps-per-turn** (App. "Number of Turns vs Turn Complexity", Fig. 13).
    Holding total operations fixed and varying K, Qwen3-32B does *worse* at low K (many simple turns);
    Gemma3-12B peaks at both extremes and fails in the middle. Cost trades off the same way: fewer
    turns, more per-turn compute.

## Method and setting

Synthetic, contamination-free **key-value dictionary task**: 100 five-letter English word keys →
integers uniform in [−99, 99]; state `S_0 = 0`; each turn supplies K keys ("the plan"); the model must
retrieve the values and report the **running sum of all turns so far** in `<answer>` tags.
Rollouts of 50,000 steps, keys sampled with replacement; 100 rollouts for Qwen3/Gemma3, 20–50 for
frontier models, identical rollouts across models. Metrics: step / turn / task accuracy, turn
complexity K, horizon length `H_s` (first step where task accuracy < s; s = 0.5 unless stated).

Models: **Qwen3 (4/8/14/32B), Gemma3 (4/12/27B)** for the scaling and self-conditioning work;
Kimi-K2, DeepSeek-V3, Qwen3-235B-Instruct-2507 as frontier *non-thinking* self-conditioning subjects;
GPT-5, Claude-4 Sonnet, Grok 4, Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-Next in the single-turn benchmark
only (via OpenRouter). Nothing is trained or fine-tuned — everything is prompting plus history
manipulation. Output caps 10k tokens (CoT) / 32k (thinking); temp 0.6/top-p 0.95 (Gemma, Qwen
thinking), 0.7/0.8 (Qwen non-thinking); temperature 0 reproduces the trends (Fig. 15). Format-following
failure fraction ≈0.1 overall and is explicitly ruled out as the primary failure mode (App. "Format
Following Failures").

Two setup facts matter enormously for transfer: **CoT traces are kept in history** (removing them made
models stop doing CoT), whereas **thinking models' `<think>` blocks are stripped from history** — the
"thinking fixes self-conditioning" result is measured in a regime where the model does *not* see its
own prior reasoning. There are **no tools, no files, no environment feedback, and no code**; the horizon
is turns of a chat, not a tool loop.

## Relevance to P1–P4

**P2 (long sessions dominate).** This is the paper's core. Our 20–200-turn tool loops with no
compaction are exactly the regime where turn accuracy is measured to fall for two separable reasons —
raw context length, and the model's own errors accumulating in that context. Result 4 says the
context-length half is largely solved at frontier scale for ~100 turns *on a clean history*; the
self-conditioning half is not solved by scale. Transfer gap: their "error in history" is a wrong
integer in an assistant message; ours is a bad edit, a refuted review finding, a failed gate — richer,
often *corrected* by an environment observation the paper's setting has no analogue for.

**P3 (every session rebuilds the picture).** Result 2 + Result 5 cut against pure re-use nostalgia:
a fresh session with a clean history is measurably the *best* history a model can have (induced error
rate 0.00 is the ceiling in every self-conditioning plot), and App. "Context Engineering" shows that
shrinking the window improves long-horizon reliability. So the per-session rebuild cost is buying
something real. The paper does not measure whether a *curated summary* of prior work preserves that
benefit — its only tested reduction is a blind sliding window over a Markovian task.

**P4 (unmeasured grounding cost of smaller reads).** The paper offers the one caveat that matters:
its context reduction works *because* the task is Markovian (state is one integer). Our slices are not;
dropping history drops grounding. It gives no measurement of that trade-off. It also supplies a cheap
instrument shape: hold the artefact fixed, vary the *error content* of the history, measure downstream
accuracy — a design we could copy on a real slice.

**P1 (context is the cost).** Only indirectly: Result 6/7 and the self-verification appendix both say
sequential thinking tokens are what buys execution length, and that they cost context — the
self-verification experiment fails *specifically* by exhausting context sooner. That is the paper's
one direct statement that a quality intervention can be net-negative through its token footprint.

## Interventions this paper supports

- **Cut a session before its own error history accumulates; hand off through a file.** Rests on
  Results 3–5 and the sliding-window appendix (accuracy improves monotonically as the window shrinks).
  Expected direction: fewer late-session errors in the longest sessions (doc-writer at 145k median /
  438k peak, big code-writer phases). Loses: everything the dropped turns grounded — the paper's own
  caveat is that the window trick needs a Markovian state, which a slice is not. Mitigate by making the
  hand-off file the state, which we already do.
- **Never re-inject a session's failed attempts, refuted findings, or reverted diffs into a successor's
  prompt.** Rests on Result 3 (monotone degradation in induced error rate) and Result 5's mechanism
  (thinking models that *don't* look back at prior answers don't self-condition). Loses: the successor
  may repeat a dead end the transcript would have warned it off — the paper measures no such cost.
- **Prefer one deep dispatch to many shallow re-dispatches of the same work.** Rests on Result 6/7
  (frontier thinking models execute hundreds of steps *in a single turn*; Claude-4 Sonnet 432) and the
  majority-voting appendix (parallel aggregation does not match sequential compute). Loses: nothing the
  paper measures — but Fig. 13 shows the optimal K is model-specific, so this is direction, not a dial.
- **Do not add a "verify your previous output" step to a dispatch prompt.** Rests directly on App.
  "Turn-wise Self-Verification": mixed-to-negative, and it accelerates context exhaustion. A
  deterministic gate (which we have) is the paper's implicit alternative — cf. their Related Work note
  that tools "shift the burden of execution from probabilistic models to reliable programs".
- **Shape tool output so the agent's *state* is re-stated explicitly rather than reconstructed.**
  Rests on App. "Deconstructing Errors": retrieval and arithmetic are near-perfect in isolation;
  the degradation is in maintaining state while doing them. Wrappers that echo the current state
  (phase, files touched, gate status) reduce the state-tracking load. The paper does not test this.

## Applicability caveats

- **No Claude Opus anywhere, and Claude-4 Sonnet only in the single-turn K benchmark** — never in a
  self-conditioning experiment. The thinking result (Result 5) is measured on **Qwen3 thinking models
  only**. Extending "thinking models don't self-condition" to Opus at high effort is an inference the
  paper does not license.
- **Thinking models were run with prior `<think>` traces stripped from history.** Our sessions retain
  prior-turn thinking (5–12 % of context tokens). If mechanism (b) is the real cause, our configuration
  is the one the paper did *not* test, and the immunity may not hold.
- **QA-shaped, not code-shaped.** No tools, no environment feedback, no self-correction opportunity;
  the authors list "task accuracy does not account for self-correction" as a limitation and note that
  in tasks where mistakes are easy to undo, self-correction is a promising direction — which is our
  setting (gates, reviews, re-runs).
- **Single-turn results are not future-proof by the authors' own admission** (summation is TC0 /
  parallelizable); the matrix-multiplication replication is the more durable version.
- Effect sizes for Results 3–5 live in figures only; this conversion carries no numeric tables.
- Nothing here needs training; every mitigation tested is prompting or history manipulation, which is
  exactly our surface.

## Briefing check

The note — "the self-conditioning result, which concerns a session's own history and so bears on
100–200-turn loops" — is **supported and well-scoped**. The measurement is literally at turn 100
(Result 3/4), the manipulated variable is literally the assistant's own prior outputs, and Result 4
shows scale does not rescue it: frontier non-thinking models "remain susceptible to self-conditioning,
as their performance consistently degrades as the induced error rate in their history increases."

Two corrections for the lead. (1) The note's implicit reassurance — that a thinking model at high
effort is immune — is only measured for **Qwen3 thinking models**, and in a configuration where prior
thinking traces were **removed from history**; our sessions keep them. Result 5's own text offers
trace-removal as one of two candidate mechanisms, so this is not a settled transfer. (2) The paper
separates two degradations that the note collapses: at 200B+ scale, **long-context degradation over
100 turns is largely solved** on a clean history — what is *not* solved is degradation caused by the
session's own mistakes. That distinction is the actionable one: a fresh context is worth it because of
what the old context *contains*, not because of how long it is.
