## Core results

Self-bias is defined as Bias(θ̂) (mean self-score minus true score) plus distance skewness (§3), measured on 6 LLMs (GPT-4, GPT-3.5-Turbo, Gemini-pro, LLaMA2-7B, Mixtral-8x7B, DeepSeekMoE-16B) across machine translation (Flores-200, 4 low-resource pairs, 100 ex./pair), CommonGen-Hard (100 ex.), and MATH (100 ex.) (§4.1).

All six models show self-bias growing across self-refine iterations on all three tasks (Figs 3, 5, 6); open-source models and GPT-3.5 amplify more than GPT-4/Gemini, but even GPT-4/Gemini amplify consistently over 10 iterations. Critically, perceived and actual quality diverge: for GPT-4/Gemini on MT, true quality (BLEURT) does not improve over 10 iterations while self-assessed quality keeps rising, so the gap grows every round (Fig 4). Human evaluation (n=50, Yor-En, iteration 0 vs. 10) confirms no real improvement despite self-reported gains — a GPT-4 case study shows self-score moving -11→0 while human score stays ≈-11 across two refinements (§4.2). In CommonGen, self-estimated coverage gains vastly outpace true coverage gains, and true performance saturates by ~iteration 5 while bias keeps climbing (Fig 5). Fluency/understandability (UniEval) improves reliably even when task quality doesn't (§4.2); models also rate their own paraphrasing style higher despite objectively worse BLEURT (Fig 8). A separate self-rewarding/best-of-k experiment (LLaMA2-7B, Mixtral, DeepSeek) shows bias rising monotonically with sample size k=1→32 (§4.3). Mitigations: external reference-based feedback (InstructScore) cuts bias and yields real gains (Fig 9); larger model size reduces bias, with LLaMA2-70B's bias plateauing after iteration 5 vs. continued amplification at 7B/13B (Fig 10).

## Relevance to A/B/C

**A** (complexity routing): weak/indirect — no task-complexity-routing experiment exists here, but the one relevant datapoint cuts against downgrading model strength for "simple" tasks: weaker models amplify self-bias more.
**B** (comment churn): directly relevant — fluency/wording improves reliably while true quality doesn't (§4.2), and models prefer outputs matching their own style over objectively better ones (Fig 8). This is the mechanism by which wording tweaks look like progress without functional value.
**C** (unbounded work amplification): directly relevant — bias/false-positive "improvement" grows monotonically with iteration count (Figs 3,4,6) and with candidate-pool size (§4.3), with no self-limiting behavior observed within tested ranges.

## Supported interventions

- **Cap refinement/review rounds.** True gains plateau (no BLEURT gain over 10 MT iterations; CommonGen saturates ~iteration 5) while perceived gains and bias keep growing — evidence for a hard cap rather than "loop until reviewer is satisfied."
- **Don't let the same model be the sole quality gate.** Self-feedback under-detects real problems and over-credits polish; external feedback both lowered bias and produced real gains (Fig 9) — supports grounding review findings in something other than the coder-model's own judgment (tests, tools, independent checks) where feasible.
- **Bound any best-of-k/multi-candidate self-selection.** Bias scales with k (§4.3).
- **Route style/wording findings separately from functional-defect findings** and treat them as low-priority/non-blocking — self-refine reliably improves the former and not the latter.
- **Keep the strongest model for review roles** rather than downgrading it; larger size measurably resisted bias amplification (Fig 10).

## Applicability caveats

No Claude models tested (GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Gemini-pro, LLaMA2, Mixtral, DeepSeek; Feb–Jun 2024, roughly two model generations old). Tasks are translation, constrained generation, and math — no code/software-engineering task, so transfer to code review/planning is inferential. Samples are small (100 ex./task, 50-example human eval) and max 10 iterations (5 for the external-feedback ablation) — no evidence on the optimal cap for a differently-shaped loop. The tested pipeline is single-agent self-refine with self-gated acceptance; our multi-role loop still shares one underlying model (Opus 5) across roles, so same-model risk plausibly transfers, but the distinct-prompt/role structure wasn't itself tested.

## Briefing check

Confirmed, with a nuance: bias amplification under iterative same-model self-refinement is precisely the paper's title and §4.2's finding. Add that the paper demonstrates the identical growth pattern in a second mechanism, self-rewarding/best-of-k sampling (§4.3) — so "iterative self-refinement" should be read as covering both round-based refinement and repeated same-model sampling/selection, not round count alone.
