## Core results

1. **Reading everything beats retrieving, for every model tested.** Averaged over 9 query-based
   datasets, LC (whole document in the prompt) beats RAG (top-5 retrieved 300-word chunks) by
   **7.6 for Gemini-1.5-Pro, 13.1 for GPT-4O, 3.6 for GPT-3.5-Turbo** (Sec 3.3, tab:benchmark rows
   \*-1 vs \*-2). The paper writes these as "%", but the k-ablation table pins them as **absolute
   points on the metric scale** (Gemini: LC 45.53 vs RAG 37.92 at k=5 → 7.61). The gap is *larger
   for the newer models*, read by the authors as long-context capability outpacing retrieval.

2. **The exception is a window overflow.** On ∞Bench En.QA/En.MC (~147k words) RAG beats LC for
   GPT-3.5-Turbo, whose window is 16k (Sec 3.3). This is the authors' one stated condition favouring
   retrieval: input "considerably exceeds the model's context window size" (Conclusion).

3. **RAG and LC mostly produce the same answer — including the same wrong answer.** Predictions
   **exactly identical for 63 % of queries**; score difference < 10 for **70 %** (Sec 4.1,
   fig:dist). The authors stress identical predictions are "not necessarily correct… the same
   correct predictions but also similar errors."

4. **Self-Route (the mechanism).** Two steps, **prompt-only, nothing trained**: (a) *RAG-and-Route* —
   query + retrieved chunks, plus the instruction "Write unanswerable if the query can not be
   answered based on the provided text"; accept the answer if it answers. (b) On a declination,
   re-run with the full context (Sec 4.2). Routing is the model's own self-reflection — no
   classifier, no retriever-score threshold.

5. **Self-Route numbers** (Sec 4.3, rows \*-3/\*-4/\*-5). Answerable by retrieval alone: **81.74 %
   Gemini-1.5-Pro**, >50 % for all three. Tokens vs LC: **38.6 % Gemini, 61 % GPT-4O**. Quality vs
   LC: **-2.2 Gemini, -0.2 GPT-4O (46.83 vs 47.04), +1.7 GPT-3.5-Turbo**; vs RAG, **+5 or better**
   for all three. The Intro's "cost reduced by 65 % for Gemini-1.5-Pro and 39 % for GPT-4O" matches
   1−0.61 for GPT-4O but overstates Gemini (1−0.386 = 61.4 %); treat the token ratios as measured.
   The authors attribute the model split to alignment: OpenAI models decline more often, buying
   accuracy with tokens.

6. **The hybrid's cost curve is non-monotonic, and it inverts.** k-ablation, Gemini, LC = 45.53
   (Appendix tab:curve):

   | top-k | RAG perf | Self-Route perf | RAG token % | Self-Route token % |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | 20.24 | 41.35 | 5.26 | 39.64 |
   | 5 | 37.92 | 43.33 | 17.02 | **38.63** |
   | 10 | 41.20 | 44.38 | 42.42 | 53.66 |
   | 50 | 44.06 | 45.19 | 95.29 | **102.97** |
   | 100 | 44.12 | 45.23 | 100.32 | **106.59** |

   Past roughly k=10 route-first **costs more than just reading everything** — you pay for the
   excerpt *and* for the full read on escalations. Cost minimum k=5; the optimum is task-dependent
   (k=1 cheapest on extractive tasks — NarrativeQA, QMSum). Self-Route's edge over plain RAG is
   largest at *small* k (k=1: 41.35 vs 20.24).

7. **Why RAG fails — four categories** (Sec 5.2, fig:failure). (A) **multi-step**: step 1's result is
   needed to know what to retrieve for step 2. (B) **general**: "What does the group think about X" —
   no formulable query. (C) **long, complex query**: hard for the retriever to parse, "arguably an
   advantage of LLMs". (D) **implicit**: the answer requires connecting the dots across the whole
   context and is never stated. Distribution tracks dataset nature: multi-hop sets (HotpotQA,
   2WikiMQA, MuSiQue) dominated by A; NarrativeQA by D; QMSum by B; "other" manually checked and
   found mostly multi-step. Provenance caveat: categories hand-derived from a sample, then **all**
   cases labelled by prompting Gemini-1.5-Pro few-shot — LLM-labelled, not human-labelled at scale.

8. **Retrieval is brittle to query phrasing; whole-context reading is not** (Sec 5.4, PassKey). RAG
   80.34 vs LC 65.25 on "What is the passkey"; reword to "What is the special token hidden inside
   the texts" and **RAG collapses to 4.58 while LC holds at 69.32**. Two passkeys, "which is
   larger": RAG 47.63 vs LC 64.24. The authors' point is that synthetic benchmarks are
   artifact-ridden; the transferable point is a 17× swing from a paraphrase.

9. **Robustness checks.** A second retriever (Dragon vs Contriever) gives consistent results
   (Sec 5.3). Suppressing parametric knowledge with "based only on the provided passage" costs
   Gemini LC 50.57 → 45.53 (Sec 5.5) — ~5 points of model knowledge switched off throughout.

## Method and setting (what was actually built/tested)

**Single-turn query-based QA and query-based summarization.** 7 LongBench sets (NarrativeQA, Qasper,
MultiFieldQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMQA, MuSiQue, QMSum; 150–200 queries each, 3.6k–18k words) plus ∞Bench
En.QA/En.MC (351/229 queries, ~150k words). Metrics F1 / accuracy / ROUGE. **Horizon: one turn** —
no tools, no agent loop, no multi-turn state, no code, no artefact produced. Models:
Gemini-1.5-Pro, GPT-4O (2024-05-13), GPT-3.5-Turbo (0125) — **all closed, none of them Claude**
(Claude 3.5 appears only in Related Work). Retrieval: Contriever and Dragon dense retrievers,
300-word chunks, top-k=5 default, ordered by similarity with the chunk index prepended. **Nothing
trained** — Self-Route is one added prompt sentence. Cost = **input token count** relative to LC; no
prices, no prompt caching, no output tokens.

## Relevance to P1–P4

**P1 (context is the cost).** Confirms the framing — the whole cost axis is input tokens. But **no
prompt caching**, the biggest arithmetic difference to us: our re-reads bill at 0.1×, so every token
ratio here is an *upper bound* on what the equivalent manoeuvre saves us.

**P2 (sessions grow; the longest dominate).** Result 6 is the load-bearing one. The doc-writer over a
whole diff and any reader of the 74 KB plan are the LC condition; "read a curated excerpt, escalate
if insufficient" is Self-Route. The k-table says that strategy has a **cost minimum at a small
excerpt and goes cost-negative once the excerpt grows** (53.66 % of LC at k=10, 102.97 % at k=50).
Transfer gap: their escalation is a second API call; ours is a new session with its own ≈33k prefix,
so our escalation is *relatively more* expensive, pushing break-even toward smaller excerpts and
lower escalation rates.

**P3 (every session rebuilds the same picture).** The 63 %-identical result argues most queries do
not need the whole document — but the paper never measures *repeated* reads: no memory, no reuse,
one query one shot. Its nearest word on our problem is result 6's inversion, which states the P3
pathology as arithmetic: a sub-agent conclusion returning into a context that then re-reads the same
files **is** the k=50 regime. That is an argument, not a measurement on our setting.

**P4 (unmeasured grounding cost of smaller reads).** The paper's core contribution and the best prior
available to us: naive curation costs **3.6–13.1 points**; the same curation plus a licence to
decline costs **0.2–2.2 points** at 39–61 % of tokens. It also gives a *predictive* account (result
7) of which tasks lose, and an instrument — the **declination rate**, directly observable per role.
Transfer gap: their quality metric is F1 against gold; we have only proxies (blocking-finding rate,
gate-red, rework share).

## Interventions this paper supports

- **Give a role a curated artefact *plus* an explicit "declare this insufficient" exit**, rather than
  either curation alone or the full artefact. Rests on results 4–5 (>50 % answerable everywhere;
  -0.2 to -2.2 vs full read). Loses: on the escalating fraction you pay twice, and Gemini's -2.2
  shows a low declination bar produces silent under-escalation.
- **Keep the excerpt a small minority of the artefact.** Result 6: minimum at k=5 (17 % of LC
  tokens), inversion by k≈10–50. Rule from the table: the hybrid wins only while
  `excerpt_fraction + escalation_rate < 1` — 0.35 at Gemini's numbers, 0.80 at a 40 % excerpt with
  40 % escalation. **Recompute in cache-adjusted tokens: at 0.1× re-reads the saving shrinks.**
- **Make sub-agent contracts two-outcome: a conclusion, or an explicit `insufficient`.** Rests on the
  Self-Route prompt — the entire mechanism is one licence-to-decline sentence, no training. Residual
  risk it does not cover: a confidently wrong conclusion (result 3), so the return must be checked by
  an independent instrument, never by agreement with a full read.
- **Do not route the roles whose task matches the failure taxonomy.** Result 7 maps near
  one-to-one: a reviewer hunting cross-cutting consequence in a diff is **D** (implicit, whole
  context); a completion consult or "is this design coherent" is **B** (general, no formulable
  query); a code-writer's orientation reads are **A** (multi-step). The route-able shape is a narrow
  lookup with a lexically matching target — a minority of what our sessions do.
- **Prefer full-file reads over similarity/grep-shaped curation when the role's task is worded
  differently from the artefact.** Result 8: a paraphrase moved retrieval 80.34 → 4.58 while whole
  reading was unmoved. Loses: full tokens.

## Applicability caveats

- **No Claude tested**, and routing behaviour is model-decisive: OpenAI declines more (61 % tokens,
  -0.2 quality), Gemini less (38.6 %, -2.2). Where Opus sits must be measured, not assumed.
- **QA, not code; one turn, not 20–200.** Nothing tests an agent doing its own iterative retrieval
  with tools — which is what our sessions are, and is exactly the remedy the authors only
  *conjecture* for category A ("engaging chain-of-thought into RAG may help", Sec 5.2).
- **Cost model has no caching and no output tokens** — a different economy from our 82 %-cache bill.
- **"Retrieval" is embedding similarity over 300-word chunks**, not a hand-authored brief or a
  file-scoped read; our curation should be higher-precision, so 3.6–13.1 points is likely a
  pessimistic bound on curation — and our escalation rate is correspondingly unknown.
- **The failure distribution is LLM-labelled**; take the taxonomy, treat proportions as indicative.
- **The suppression prompt cuts ~5 points** of parametric knowledge (result 9). We *want* the model's
  prior knowledge of repo conventions, so absolute scores understate a knowledge-carrying reader.
- Contexts top out near 150k words; our longest sessions (438k tokens) exceed the tested range.

## Briefing check

The note — *"extract when long context beats retrieval and at what cost; both studies are QA, not
code, so transfer to plans and diffs is the open question"* — holds on both halves and does not
overstate. Long context wins: "LC consistently outperforms RAG for all the three models, with a
significant margin… 7.6 % for Gemini-1.5-Pro, 13.1 % for GPT-4O, and 3.6 % for GPT-3.5-Turbo"
(Sec 3.3). The QA-only caveat is if anything understated: all nine datasets are single-turn, the
horizon is one call, and no tool loop or code artefact appears anywhere. On "at what cost": cost is
raw input tokens with **no caching**, so the ratios cannot be carried into our bill unchanged.

Two things the note misses and the lead should carry instead of the headline gap: (1) the
decision-relevant result is the **non-monotonic cost curve**, not "LC > RAG" — route-first is cheaper
only while the excerpt stays a small minority, and at k=50 it costs 103 % of reading everything;
(2) the **failure taxonomy** is what actually makes the transfer argument to plans and diffs, and it
lands *against* routing our reviewer and doc-writer while allowing it for narrow lookups.

**Transfer answer for the lead.** The paper supports letting the role decide per task only where the
task is lookup-shaped and the excerpt is small; it argues against it for the reviewer and doc-writer,
whose jobs are its category-D and category-B failures by construction. The kill signal is arithmetic
and needs no quality judgment: instrument **escalation rate** and **excerpt fraction** in
cache-adjusted tokens and kill the scheme when the excerpt plus the escalations cost as much as one
full read — the k=50 row is what that looks like after it has failed. The quality kill signal must be
an independent instrument (blocking-finding rate, gate-red, rework share), never agreement between
routed and full-read answers: result 3 shows the two agree 63 % of the time *including on errors*.
