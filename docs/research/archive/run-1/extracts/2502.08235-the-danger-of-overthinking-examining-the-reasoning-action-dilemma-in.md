## Core results

Defines *overthinking in agentic settings* as favoring internal simulation over environmental
interaction (the Reasoning-Action Dilemma), and measures it on SWE-bench Verified via the
OpenHands/CodeAct scaffold: 19 models (o1, o1-mini, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o, DeepSeek-R1/V3,
QwQ-32B, Qwen2.5 and R1-distill families, 1.5B–671B), 3,908–4,018 trajectories, each scored 0–10 by
a Claude 3.5 Sonnet judge (temperature 0, outcome-blind) for three patterns: **Analysis Paralysis**
(planning instead of acting), **Rogue Actions** (chained actions without awaiting feedback),
**Premature Disengagement** (concluding from internal simulation). Scores validated against 4 human
expert annotators.

- Overthinking correlates negatively with issue resolution for both model classes (regression
  β = −7.894 reasoning, −15.938 non-reasoning; §5.1–5.2). Reasoning models overthink ~1.6× more
  than non-reasoning (3.505 vs 2.228 mean score), but non-reasoning models are hurt *more* per
  unit of overthinking.
- **Cost result (§5.6):** o1-high resolves 29.1% at $1,400; o1-low 21.0% at $400. Generating two
  o1-low solutions and keeping the one with the **lower overthinking score** yields 27.3% at $800
  (43% cheaper than high); k=3 yields 30.3%, *surpassing* o1-high at 15% lower cost.
- **Against the token-count proxy (§5.4):** o1-low shows **35% higher** overthinking scores than
  o1-high (2.774 vs 2.426, significant) — in agentic settings, *more* reasoning tokens can reduce
  overthinking. Overthinking here is about ignoring environment feedback, not thinking long.
- Smaller models overthink more (§5.3); context window size is uncorrelated (§5.5). Native
  function calling drops o1's overthinking 2.43→1.05 and lifts resolution 29.1%→47.7% (§6.1).

## Relevance to A/B/C

- **A — direct.** The strongest agentic-setting evidence that (i) process-quality selection over k
  cheap attempts beats one expensive attempt, and (ii) effort level and overthinking are *not* the
  same axis — lowering effort alone is not a fix and can worsen the paralysis pattern.
- **B — none.**
- **C — methodological.** The outcome-blind trajectory judge (score process failure modes without
  seeing success) is a reusable instrument pattern for auditing our planner/reviewer transcripts.

## Supported interventions

1. Best-of-k with process-quality selection (catalogue A5): k=2 low-effort attempts + selection
   matched/beat high effort at 57% cost — measured on real SWE tasks, the closest domain to ours.
2. An overthinking-style process metric over planner transcripts (planning-to-action ratio,
   actions per turn) as instrumentation — the score correlates with outcomes and with human
   judgment (catalogue I2/I3 flavor).
3. Do **not** treat "fewer reasoning tokens" as the goal (contradicts naive budget-capping): the
   o1-low result shows effort reduction can increase agentic overthinking (informs catalogue A3's
   risk paragraph).

## Applicability caveats

Models are early-2025 era (o1, R1, Claude 3.5 Sonnet); single-agent scaffold, not a multi-role
loop; the overthinking score is itself an LLM judgment (validated, but n=20 human-rated traces);
SWE-bench Verified tasks are self-contained repo issues, smaller than our slices. The selection
result needs k independent full attempts — directly affordable for plans, expensive for whole
slices.

## Briefing check

Confirmed as the S1 anchor: the taxonomy (analysis paralysis / rogue actions / premature
disengagement) and the quantified selection result are exactly as advertised. One important
addition the briefing misses: the paper *refutes* the "long thinking = overthinking" equation for
agentic work (§5.4) — its overthinking is environmental-feedback neglect, which means our problem-A
diagnosis should look at what the planner *does* (act/read ratio), not only at how many tokens it
burns.
