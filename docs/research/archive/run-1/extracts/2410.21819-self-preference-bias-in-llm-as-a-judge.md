## Core results

**Self-preference bias (pairwise judging).** Using Definition 1 (an Equal-Opportunity-style metric: recall on self-generated pairs minus recall on other-generated pairs, both conditioned on human preference), GPT-4 showed the largest bias among 8 judges: recall 0.945 on human-favored self-outputs vs. 0.425 on human-disfavored self-outputs, bias = 0.520 (Section 4, Fig. 3/4). Vicuna-13b and Koala-13b also biased; oasst-pythia-12b, dolly-v2-12b, stablelm-tuned-alpha-7b showed *negative* bias (self-underrating). A Demographic-Parity variant (Eq. 4, Table 2) confirms ranking: GPT-4 0.749, Vicuna-13b 0.382, GPT-3.5-turbo 0.191, Koala-13b 0.175, Vicuna-7b 0.052, oasst-pythia-12b 0.006, dolly/stablelm slightly negative. Dataset: Chatbot Arena, ~33,000 open-ended dialogue pairs, human-labeled.

**Perplexity/familiarity mechanism (Section 5).** Restricted to 6 open-weight judges (GPT-4/3.5 excluded — no accessible logprobs): all but stablelm-tuned-alpha-7b rate lower-perplexity responses higher, more strongly than humans do. Critically, splitting judgments into "self-output included" vs. "not included" pairs, the perplexity-vs-rating curves are near-identical for 4/6 models — i.e., *self-authorship label barely matters once perplexity is controlled*. Own outputs simply tend to have lower perplexity (Fig. 5). Authors' causal claim: "the essence of the bias lies in perplexity... LLMs prefer texts more familiar to them," independent of whether they authored the text. A Llama2/Llama3.1 follow-up (no human labels for those models' own outputs, so only judge-vs-human perplexity-sensitivity is shown, not self-vs-other) corroborates that perplexity-driven over-sensitivity persists in newer, competitive open models.

Discussion notes GPT-4's bias often reflects *style* differences (hedging/disclaimers, framing before answering), not factual quality — consistent with policy/alignment-driven, not fact-driven, preference.

## Relevance to A/B/C

- **A (planner deep-dive scaling): none.** Paper doesn't touch generation depth/effort or complexity routing.
- **B (comment churn, "will"→"may"): directly relevant.** The mechanism predicts exactly this: a reviewer prefers phrasing low-perplexity *to itself*, independent of who wrote it. Hedging/qualifier tweaks are precisely the kind of stylistic, non-factual "improvement" the paper documents (Discussion: bias tracks "handling of specialized domains or description of premises," not errors).
- **C (unbounded work amplification): relevant.** Because the preference target is the judge's own familiar phrasing distribution (not a fixed correctness bar), each "fair-looking" finding can still miss the judge's true attractor after a fix, motivating further findings — a plausible driver of unbounded churn, though the paper doesn't measure multi-round fix-then-refind dynamics.

## Supported interventions

1. **Downweight/discount reviewer findings correlated with low target-text perplexity to the reviewer**, per the paper's own proposed mitigation (Discussion: weight down a model's judgment on samples where it shows low perplexity) — apply specifically to comment-wording/style findings.
2. **Require cross-model or ensemble corroboration before treating a stylistic finding as blocking**, backed by the ensemble-evaluation mitigation the authors propose to counter single-model bias (Discussion).
3. **Don't rely on model/session variation alone to neutralize bias** — since bias tracked perplexity not self-authorship-label (Section 5's near-identical self/other curves), a same-base-model reviewer likely shares the coder's low-perplexity attractor even without being "the same instance," suggesting genuinely different-model review matters more than prompt/session separation for stylistic findings.
4. **Use the paper's Demographic-Parity-style metric as an internal audit**: sample reviewer accept/reject decisions on paired phrasings to check whether "quality" findings are perplexity-driven vs. substance-driven.

## Applicability caveats

Judges tested: GPT-4/3.5-Turbo, Vicuna, Koala, Dolly, oasst-pythia, StableLM, plus a Llama2/3.1 add-on — no Claude models, nothing near current frontier capability. The core perplexity mechanism (Section 5) was measured only on the smaller open models, *not* on GPT-4 (the model with the largest measured bias) — its perplexity was inaccessible, so the causal link for GPT-4 is argued by analogy, not directly shown. Task domain is open-ended chatbot dialogue quality, not code or code comments. The bias metric is single-turn pairwise preference, not a multi-round raise-then-fix workflow, so "work amplification" (C) is an extrapolation, not something the paper measures. Published Oct 2024 (updated Jun 2025); pre-dates current reasoning-tuned/agentic coding models.

## Briefing check

Nuance it: the paper's own claim is that perplexity/familiarity *is* the mechanism and self-preference is its downstream symptom, not a separate "self-recognition" phenomenon — the judge isn't detecting "this is mine," it's reacting to familiarity that happens to correlate with self-authorship. Better phrasing: "familiarity (perplexity) preference, of which self-preference is one observable symptom rather than an independent recognition-based mechanism."
