## Core results

Summarization (XSUM + CNN/DailyMail, 1,000 articles each) with GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Llama-2-7b as
generator/evaluator/identifier roles:

- **Out-of-the-box self-recognition is real:** all three models exceed 50% pairwise accuracy at
  picking their own summary; GPT-4 hits 73.5% distinguishing itself from two other LLMs and
  humans. Weaker models can't distinguish themselves from stronger ones.
- **Self-preference is present without human-preference justification:** pairwise self-preference
  scores of any two evaluators sum to >1 (both can't be right); all models most strongly
  disfavor the *human* summary.
- **The causal result:** fine-tuning on as few as 500 examples drives self-recognition >90%, and
  across all fine-tuned variants (including control tasks — length, vowel count, readability,
  constant, random) **self-preference strength is linearly correlated with self-recognition
  capability** (Fig. 1); example-level correlation (Kendall's τ 0.37–0.82). Reverse causality
  rejected: evaluators don't prefer fine-tuned models' outputs (mean 0.46).
- **Labels flip preference:** attaching (or swapping) authorship labels reverses GPT-4/GPT-3.5's
  preference on XSUM — they prefer whatever is *labeled* theirs.
- **Ordering bias is large:** preference reverses under option swap for 25% (GPT-4), 58%
  (GPT-3.5), 89% (Llama-2) of pairs — single-order pairwise judgments are unreliable.

## Relevance to A/B/C

- **Judge design (Q6) — central.** Same-model evaluation is structurally biased, and the bias
  runs through recognition/familiarity of the text. Combined with Wataoka et al. (2410.21819),
  the mechanism is familiarity, so register/session separation of the same base model does not
  remove it.
- **B — supporting.** Reviewer preferences about *how something is phrased* are exactly where
  self-similarity bias lives.
- **A/C — none directly.**

## Supported interventions

1. **Provenance hiding / no authorship signals** in anything judged (labels demonstrably flip
   preference) — catalogue D2/D3.
2. **Order randomization + swap-consistency** for any pairwise judgment (25–89% reversal rates
   make single-order comparisons meaningless) — D3.
3. Treat same-model stylistic verdicts as low-information (B4/D1) — the evaluator's "better" is
   substantially "more like me."

## Applicability caveats

Summarization only; 2024 models (GPT-4/3.5, Llama-2-7b); causality is supported by controls, not
proven mechanistically; disproportionality vs. true quality is only indirectly established (the
scores-sum->1 argument). A related study (Koo et al.) found the opposite GPT-4/3.5 ordering,
so magnitudes are dataset-sensitive.

## Briefing check

Confirmed for "self-preference/self-recognition." Read together with Wataoka et al.: recognition
and familiarity are two lenses on the same attractor — this paper shows the capability→bias link
and the label-flip; Wataoka shows the perplexity mechanism that survives even when authorship is
irrelevant. Both converge on the same practical rule: don't let phrasing-level preferences of a
same-model judge carry decision weight.
