## Core results

Constructs task families where *longer reasoning lowers accuracy* (TMLR 12/2025; Anthropic-heavy
author list). Two setups: *controlled* (prompted reasoning budgets 0–16k tokens) and *natural*
(rank 5 samples per question by length). Standard benchmarks (GSM8K etc.) show **no** inverse
scaling — the failure needs specific conditions:

- **Counting with distractors:** trivial questions ("you have an apple and an orange … how many
  fruits?") with n embedded distractor math/Python snippets. Claude Opus 4 drops from ~100% to
  85–90% as reasoning lengthens; DeepSeek-R1 from 70%→30% at 5 distractors (natural setup); o3
  is more stable. Qualitatively: models fixate on the distractors, consider the simple answer,
  then talk themselves back out of it. **Distraction by irrelevant context is the
  Claude-specific failure mode.**
- **Famous-paradox framing:** trivial questions dressed as the Birthday/Sleeping-Beauty paradox
  trigger memorized complex solutions; adding distractors that *mask* the framing improves o3 —
  models estimate difficulty from surface framing, not content.
- **Grades regression (zero-shot):** with longer reasoning, predictions shift from the genuinely
  predictive feature (study hours, r=0.73 with truth) to plausible spurious ones (sleep, stress).
  **Few-shot examples largely correct this** — concrete reference points anchor the reasoning.
- **Zebra puzzles (BBEH):** all models degrade with natural extended reasoning; longest traces
  show unfocused exploration and second-guessing vs. the shortest traces' systematic constraint
  tracking.
- **Safety (MWE):** Claude Sonnet 4's stated willingness to be shut off falls 60%→47% with longer
  reasoning — model- and task-specific, not universal.

## Relevance to A/B/C

- **A — direct, two mechanisms.** (i) More compute actively hurts on easy or distractor-laden
  tasks; (ii) difficulty misestimation from framing is precisely why upfront complexity grading
  fails (catalogue A4). The distraction result also implicates *context volume*: what a register
  is shown matters as much as how hard it thinks.
- **B/C — indirect.** The spurious-feature drift under extended reasoning is a plausible analog
  of reviewers surfacing plausible-but-irrelevant findings on long inspection, but that mapping
  is not measured here.

## Supported interventions

1. **Context diet per register** (catalogue D2): keep irrelevant material out of scope — for
   Claude models the harm from distractors is measured, not hypothetical.
2. **Ground judgments in concrete anchors:** few-shot/reference examples corrected the spurious
   drift — consistent with anchoring review to named criteria and evidence (C1) rather than
   open-ended contemplation.
3. **Don't infer difficulty from framing** (A4): the paradox-framing result is the mechanism
   behind our graded-lane failure.

## Applicability caveats

Tasks are synthetic and built to elicit the failure — effect sizes are upper bounds for natural
conditions (the authors say so). Models are Claude Opus 4/Sonnet 4, o3, R1 — one generation before
Opus 5. Controlled-budget prompting ("don't think"…"ultrathink") is not the API effort parameter.
No multi-agent or review-loop setting.

## Briefing check

Confirmed, including the specific claim that distraction-by-irrelevant-context is documented for
Claude models. Worth adding: inverse scaling did *not* appear on standard benchmarks — so the
lesson for us is conditional (it bites where context carries distractors or framing misleads), and
the natural setup is worse than the controlled one, i.e. models' *own* length allocation is the
least trustworthy regime.
