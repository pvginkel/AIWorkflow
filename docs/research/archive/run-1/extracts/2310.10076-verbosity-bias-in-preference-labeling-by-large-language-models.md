## Core results

The paper runs two experiments, both using GPT-4 (and GPT-3.5 for one metric) as judge, with answers generated by Vicuna-7b-v1.5 and human labels from Anthropic's HH-RLHF dataset.

**Exp. 1 (Section "Verbosity Preference of LLMs"):** 100 samples × 3 "creative" prompts (travel blog, email, product-comparison blog, from Zheng et al.'s MT-bench category). GPT-4 pairwise-judged answers (position-swapped to control position bias). Result: positive correlation between word-count difference and preference score; when the length gap is large, GPT-4 "almost always" picks the longer answer.

**Exp. 2 (Section "Is There A Difference in Verbosity Preference Between LLMs and Humans?"):** Using HH-RLHF human labels as ground truth, GPT-4's agreement with humans ("human alignment") was high when humans *also* preferred the longer answer, but dropped sharply when humans preferred the *shorter* one — GPT-4 kept choosing the longer answer regardless of human judgment.

**Proposed metric (Section "Formulation of Verbosity Bias"):** verbosity bias defined via accuracy-parity fairness math (Hardt et al. 2016), treating "which side is longer" as the sensitive attribute; bias = difference in judge inaccuracy rate between the "longer=preferred" and "longer=not-preferred" groups. Measured values (Table): **GPT-4 = 0.328, GPT-3.5 = 0.428** — GPT-4 better but still substantially biased, contradicting the more optimistic reading of Wang et al. 2023's narrower list-repetition test (GPT-4 <10% attack success vs. GPT-3.5/Claude-v1 >90%, cited, not this paper's own data).

## Relevance to A/B/C

- **A (planner sizing effort by task): none.** Paper doesn't touch task-complexity routing.
- **B (comment churn): relevant, indirectly.** The paper's core mechanism — comparative/preference judges over-reward length even absent quality gain — is exactly the pathway by which a reviewer/judge could implicitly reward a coder for more prose/comments. It's suggestive, not proven for code: the paper never tests code or comments, only prose QA/chat answers.
- **C (unbounded work amplification): indirect.** The paper shows one-shot pairwise bias, not iterative review dynamics. It offers a plausible *directional* mechanism (each length-favoring judgment nudges output longer) but doesn't test multi-round amplification.

## Supported interventions

- **Length-normalize/control comparative judgments** — directly modeled on the paper's own design (bin by % length difference, observe correlation with score); apply this diagnostic to our reviewer prompts.
- **Periodically audit reviewers with an accuracy-parity-style bias check** (Section "Formulation of Verbosity Bias") — stratify judge decisions by which candidate is longer, check for a skew independent of content.
- **Prefer chain-of-thought / evidence-first judging** (Preliminaries: "Chain-of-thought Prompting," "Multiple Evidence Calibration") — cited mitigations for judgment biases generally.
- **Favor absolute/checklist rubrics over side-by-side "pick the better one" framings** where feasible — supported by the cited Huang et al. finding that verbosity bias appeared in comparison grading but not single-answer grading.

## Applicability caveats

Models tested are GPT-4/GPT-3.5 (Oct 2023) — no Claude data at all. Tasks are open-ended creative writing and HH-RLHF assistant chat, not code or code review. Candidate answers came from a small, dated open model (Vicuna-7b). Sample sizes are small (100×3 prompts; HH-RLHF bracket counts unstated in text). The paper's own limitations section flags that the accuracy-parity metric can mask within-group bias shape. Work is nearly three years old and pre-agentic; no test of comments, diffs, or iterative multi-round review.

## Briefing check

Confirmed, with a nuance: the paper is squarely about LLM-judge verbosity bias in pairwise preference labeling, matching "verbosity bias in evaluators." But note the direction isn't universal — the paper itself cites Huang et al. finding the opposite bias (short-preferred) in summarization — so the briefing should treat "prefers longer" as task-dependent, not a fixed property of evaluators.
